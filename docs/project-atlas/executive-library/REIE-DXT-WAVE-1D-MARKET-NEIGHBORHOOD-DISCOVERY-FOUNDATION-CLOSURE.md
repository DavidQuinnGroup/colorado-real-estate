## 3. Market Contract Finding

The certified Market contract establishes Market pages as concise customer briefings.

Future Market pages should help customers answer:

- What is happening in this market?
- What evidence matters to the customer's decision?
- What is directional versus verified?
- What should the customer investigate next?
- Which property, neighborhood, or advisory journey should continue?

The certified foundation does not define Market pages as internal KPI dashboards, governance reports, raw data dumps, predictive certainty engines, investment recommendation systems, valuation systems, ranking systems, or forecast products.

## 4. Neighborhood Contract Finding

The certified Neighborhood contract establishes Neighborhood pages as orientation to place before dense evidence.

Future Neighborhood pages should help customers answer:

- What kind of place is this?
- How is it organized geographically?
- What housing context is available?
- What should the customer verify personally or professionally?
- Which properties, markets, or advisory resources should continue?

The certified foundation requires Neighborhood experience work to orient customers to place before presenting inventory density, market context, visual intelligence, or evidence-heavy sections.

## 5. Shared Hierarchy Finding

The certified shared documentation-level hierarchy is:

1. Place orientation
2. Governing customer question
3. Concise customer promise
4. Scope-specific context themes
5. Tool or evidence continuation
6. Questions to verify
7. Evidence, freshness, limitation, and professional boundaries
8. Transition to Search, Property, Buyer, Seller, or Advisory journey
9. Compact next-decision continuations

This hierarchy is not a runtime abstraction, schema, component framework, route registry, or shared UI implementation.

## 6. Fair-Housing And Evidence Boundary Finding

The certified Neighborhood foundation prohibits:

- protected-class steering;
- demographic suitability conclusions;
- best-neighborhood claims;
- safety guarantees;
- school-quality conclusions;
- investment guarantees;
- predictive appreciation claims;
- personalized suitability rankings.

The certified Market foundation prohibits:

- predictive certainty;
- valuation certainty;
- investment recommendations;
- ranking claims;
- forecast conclusions;
- provider activation;
- customer-specific suitability conclusions.

Evidence, limitation, freshness, and professional-boundary language must appear at the point where customers are asked to rely on market or neighborhood context.
