## 7. Initiatives Evaluated

### Controlled Evidence Depth Integration

Finding: highest-value next implementation candidate.

The Evidence Depth foundation is certified, internal, deterministic, fixture-backed, read-only, and conclusion-free. The highest-value next step is to integrate it into one bounded existing domain so the foundation begins producing reusable governance value without activating providers or public conclusions.

The safest target is internal Advisory preparation: a fixture-backed, non-public integration that maps evidence posture concepts to advisory question-preparation categories and limitation prompts. This uses the newly certified Advisory Handoff path without changing public pages, Contact behavior, CRM, tracking, or customer data.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture

Finding: strategically important, not primary now.

Neighborhood and submarket intelligence has high customer, SEO, and authority value, but architecture work remains blocked by object-type policy, canonical identity, neighborhood/subdivision/district/market-area/corridor/unincorporated-community distinctions, Gunbarrel-like ambiguity, route and registry governance, evidence requirements, fair-housing controls, and certification scale. It should follow controlled evidence integration or proceed only as a separately authorized architecture-only program.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: valuable but premature as the next primary initiative.

Evidence Depth makes property and seller evidence categories more feasible, but this area has higher risk because property identity, title, condition, insurance, disclosure, structural, environmental, ownership, privacy, valuation, and professional-boundary issues can drift into customer-specific conclusions. It should follow a broader controlled Evidence Depth integration unless scoped strictly internal and fixture-backed.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: not the next priority.

Mixed-maturity comparison remains certified. Evidence Depth increases the future value of clearer evidence transparency, but it does not require Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette to be flattened, relabeled, or migrated into `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` now. Editorial depth should be preserved until customer or operational evidence shows mixed maturity is materially confusing or expensive to govern.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

Niwot still presents authority, object-type, route, registry, search-support, and unincorporated-community questions. The evidence and geographic records suggest Niwot is more likely a neighborhood/submarket or geographic-object governance question than a simple city expansion. It should remain paused until geographic architecture resolves the object-class question.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: no broad product-experience wave is justified.

The current public product layer has recently certified cohesion, comparison, seller readiness, buyer financing readiness, and advisory handoff. A small targeted product improvement should occur only if production evidence shows specific discovery, CTA, mobile, duplicate-path, or cognitive-load friction. No such repository evidence currently outranks Evidence Depth integration.

### Advisory Operating Readiness

Finding: strong secondary candidate, but should not outrun Evidence Depth integration.

Internal advisory operating readiness has meaningful business leverage. However, without a first controlled Evidence Depth integration, it risks becoming generic operating documentation. A later advisory operating framework should reuse the controlled integration output, remain non-customer-specific, and avoid CRM, tracking, lead routing, profiling, and workflow activation.

### Other Repository-Supported Priorities

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks Controlled Evidence Depth Integration after Advisory Handoff closure.
