## 5. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

The remaining highest-value gap is seller decision readiness.

Customers can now orient, search, understand city context, compare markets, read buyer and seller guidance, understand financing boundaries, use Grand Plan, and reach Advisory. A homeowner considering a sale still needs a more structured readiness path between:

- "What is my home worth?"
- "What should I prepare or document before a pricing conversation?"
- "Which property, condition, market, and timing questions should I organize?"
- "How do I get advisor review without receiving an unsupported automated value?"

The business gap is similar: seller conversion value is high, but the next implementation must avoid drifting into valuation conclusions, price predictions, automated pricing, appraisal substitution, or seller-lead automation. The opportunity is readiness and preparation, not valuation output.

## 6. Initiatives Evaluated

### Seller Readiness Advancement

Finding: highest-priority next implementation candidate.

Seller readiness best uses the newly certified product journey. It can connect Sell, Home Worth, Market Context, Property Intelligence, Grand Plan, and Advisory through a protected-boundary-safe readiness experience. The correct first scope is not automated valuation. The safer scope is preparation guidance, documentation inventory, property-context prompts, market-context continuation, and advisory next steps.

### Buyer Financing Readiness Advancement

Finding: strong second priority, but more compliance-sensitive.

Buyer financing readiness can reuse Buyer Guidance, Financing Confidence, Search, Grand Plan, and Advisory. It should remain education-first and avoid calculators, rates, qualification, personal financial intake, lender recommendations, or affordability conclusions. Its regulatory and advertising boundaries make it slightly less suitable than seller readiness for the immediate next implementation.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: useful later, not urgent.

Mixed-maturity comparison is now certified, which reduces the need to unify Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette immediately. Unification may simplify validation and comparison semantics later, but it is not a prerequisite for current customer value and could flatten existing editorial depth if handled mechanically.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence

Finding: strategically important but not ready now.

Neighborhood and submarket intelligence would create high customer and local-authority value, but object-type governance, route eligibility, fair-housing controls, evidence requirements, classification of ambiguous entities such as Gunbarrel, and certification scale remain material blockers.

### Evidence Depth And Data Integration

Finding: enterprise-critical but not the next public implementation.

Evidence depth can improve reliability, freshness, provenance, and public-record support. Current source-rights and Geographic Intelligence records keep provider activation, GIS activation, public-record ingestion, customer-facing activation, and production writes gated. A read-only evidence readiness phase remains valuable, but seller readiness can proceed without activating new evidence sources if it stays advisory, non-valuation, and limitation-forward.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

The public `FOUNDATION` backlog is complete. Niwot still requires authority, object-type, route, and search-support reconciliation. Its incremental customer value is narrower than seller readiness, and activation should not proceed until governance resolves the unincorporated-community and search-support questions.

### Product Experience Additional Cohesion Or Discovery

Finding: not justified as the immediate next implementation.

The recent cohesion wave and Cross-City Comparison both improved discovery and continuity. Another broad product-experience pass would have diminishing returns unless production analytics, customer feedback, or certification evidence identifies a specific new friction point.

### Other Repository-Supported Priorities

The repository contains credible longer-term programs across Geographic Intelligence, enterprise adapters, source-rights readiness, neighborhood products, buyer financing, and seller confidence. None currently outranks a protected-boundary-safe seller readiness advancement for immediate customer value, business leverage, architecture reuse, implementation velocity, and certifiability.
