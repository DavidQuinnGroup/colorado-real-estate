## 5. Post-Wave Assessment

The Neighborhood / Submarket foundation is now sufficiently validated for future bounded internal waves, but a second similar internal wave is not the highest-value next implementation unless a concrete activation or route-enhancement target is selected.

Existing public neighborhood routes are better governed than before, but the review did not identify a specific existing route that is ready for bounded public enhancement without additional content, evidence, source-rights, responsive, public-copy, and fair-housing certification work.

New public neighborhood routes remain premature. The first wave preserved routes and proved readiness posture; it did not create route eligibility, registry eligibility, public evidence posture, public content standards, Search support, map readiness, or public activation authority for new routes.

Niwot governance is better framed by the certified object-type architecture, but remains unresolved and blocked. Gunbarrel remains blocked because repository evidence continues to show object-type ambiguity and same-name city/neighborhood/market context conflict.

Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 should remain paused because its likely candidates still carry object-type, route, Search, evidence, source-rights, and certification blockers.

Another standalone internal architecture phase would create diminishing returns. REIE now has certified customer surfaces, advisory/readiness layers, evidence foundations, geographic architecture, Decision Guide transparency, and first-wave neighborhood governance. The next initiative should apply those foundations to the largest remaining business/customer gap.

## 6. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

Largest remaining customer-value gap: property and seller evidence readiness.

The public seller, Home Worth, property, advisory, and Decision Guide surfaces are certified and limitation-forward, but the platform still lacks a governed internal evidence-category foundation for property and seller preparation. Customers and advisors need a safer way to reason about what documents, source categories, rights, freshness, conflicts, and professional review areas matter before any future property-specific or seller-specific intelligence can be considered.

Material gaps:

- Seller Readiness identifies documentation and verification needs but does not yet have an internal evidence-category foundation.
- Home Worth remains intentionally non-valuation, but future seller preparation still needs controlled evidence posture around title, permits, improvements, disclosures, insurance, HOA, municipal, structural, environmental, and specialist-review categories.
- Property Intelligence is certified as a public decision workspace, but deeper property evidence remains constrained by privacy, ownership, public-record, provider, rights, valuation, and condition-conclusion boundaries.
- Advisory Operating Readiness can organize preparation, but it should not infer property/seller evidence posture without a domain contract.
- Neighborhood work has now completed architecture and one internal wave; a second wave would mostly extend coverage unless attached to a specific route or activation problem.

## 7. Initiatives Evaluated

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: strongest next implementation priority.

Property / Seller Evidence Readiness can create the highest combined customer, business, advisory, and enterprise value if kept internal, fixture-backed, non-customer-specific, non-persistent, and conclusion-free. It should define evidence categories, source-rights posture, freshness, provenance, conflict handling, professional review boundaries, privacy boundaries, and fail-closed activation rules for property and seller preparation without activating lookup, records, uploads, providers, valuation, condition conclusions, or customer-specific analysis.

This is now timely because Seller Readiness, Home Worth, Property Intelligence, Evidence Depth, Controlled Evidence, Advisory Operating, and source-rights governance are already certified.

### Second Governed Neighborhood / Submarket Wave

Finding: useful later, but not the next primary implementation.

A second internal wave could expand candidate inventory, deepen identity and relationship reconciliation, add preservation cases, and improve evidence/source-rights readiness. However, without a specific public route enhancement or activation target, it risks repeating the first wave at lower marginal value.

### Existing Neighborhood Route Enhancement

Finding: not ready as the next primary initiative.

Bounded public enhancement of existing routes may become valuable through maturity and limitation transparency, evidence-boundary copy, and stronger city/property continuity. Current records show route preservation and evidence posture, not enough public-copy, responsive, evidence, source-rights, and fair-housing readiness to recommend a public enhancement now.

### New Public Neighborhood Routes

New public route creation still requires separate route design, registry eligibility, Search support, map/boundary rights where applicable, content standard, source-rights posture, evidence completeness, fair-housing review, responsive review, deterministic certification, production certification, and executive authorization.

### Gunbarrel Governance Reconciliation

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4
