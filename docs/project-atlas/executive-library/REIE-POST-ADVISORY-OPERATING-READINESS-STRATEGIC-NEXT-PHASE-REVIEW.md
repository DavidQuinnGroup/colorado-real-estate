## 6. Diminishing-Returns Finding

Finding: another standalone internal foundation would create diminishing returns unless it is attached to a concrete product or geographic domain need.

Evidence Depth has been proven through an internal Advisory preparation integration. Advisory Operating Readiness has converted that proof into a human-use standard. A second generic evidence integration or another abstract operating layer would mostly duplicate certified proof unless it directly supports Neighborhood/Submarket, Property/Seller, Decision Guide, or Local Decision Intelligence governance.

## 7. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

The largest remaining gap between REIE capability and customer value is geographic depth below city intelligence.

The current product can guide customers across city-level markets, buyer/seller readiness, comparison, and advisory preparation. It is weaker at explaining governed local geography below cities: neighborhoods, subdivisions, districts, corridors, market areas, and unincorporated communities.

Material gaps:

- Neighborhood and submarket object types are not yet governed for scalable architecture.
- Gunbarrel, Niwot, and similar places remain ambiguous across city, neighborhood, market-area, and unincorporated-community classifications.
- Parent/child geographic relationships are not ready for customer-facing use.
- Existing neighborhood knowledge is distributed across registries, property fields, market pages, internal links, map fixtures, and SEO structures.
- Source-rights, provenance, freshness, conflict, and fair-housing controls need to be applied to sub-city geography before public expansion.
- Long-term local authority and SEO growth require a governed 10,000-neighborhood-scale architecture, not another isolated public page.

## 8. Initiatives Evaluated

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture

Finding: strongest next implementation priority.

The initiative has the highest combined customer, SEO, local-authority, strategic, and enterprise value now that public journey, evidence, and operating standards are certified. It should be architecture-only and should not create public routes. The safe first scope is object-type governance, canonical identity, parent/child relationships, route and registry standards, evidence requirements, source-rights integration, fair-housing controls, certification scale, and sequencing toward future public implementation.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: valuable secondary candidate, but not first.

Evidence Depth and Advisory Operating make this more feasible, and Property/Seller evidence would support Seller Readiness, Home Worth, Property Intelligence, and Advisory preparation. It remains higher-risk because property identity, address/ownership handling, public records, provider dependencies, uploads, valuation, appraisal-substitution, condition, title, insurance, disclosure, permit, environmental, municipal, HOA, and specialist boundaries are closer to customer-specific or professional conclusions.

### Decision Guide Evidence Transparency

Finding: useful later and more valuable than full unification, but not the next primary initiative.

Evidence transparency could help Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette preserve editorial depth while making evidence limitations more explicit. It should be bounded and non-scoring. It does not outrank Neighborhood/Submarket because sub-city object governance is now the larger architectural blocker for future city, guide, neighborhood, and comparison work.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: still not justified as a primary initiative.

Mixed-maturity comparison is governed and certified. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette should not be mechanically migrated to `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` if that would reduce editorial depth. Unification should wait for evidence of customer confusion, governance drag, or a concrete integration requirement.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 / Niwot Reconciliation

Finding: remain paused.

Niwot and Gunbarrel should be resolved through Neighborhood/Submarket object governance before any city-level expansion. Treating them as simple city candidates would bypass the needed classification, parent/child, route, registry, evidence, search, and fair-housing decisions.

### Second Domain-Specific Evidence Integration

Finding: do not run as a standalone proof program.

The first controlled integration already proved the Evidence Depth foundation. Future evidence integration should be embedded inside the selected domain, especially Neighborhood/Submarket or Property/Seller Evidence, instead of becoming a separate generic program.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: not justified by current production evidence.

Product Experience cohesion, Comparison, Seller Readiness, Buyer Financing Readiness, Advisory Handoff, and Advisory Operating are certified. A targeted improvement should wait for specific production evidence of discoverability, CTA, mobile, duplicate-path, cognitive-load, or conversion-continuity friction.

### Other Repository-Supported Priority

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks Neighborhood/Submarket Intelligence Architecture after Advisory Operating Readiness closure.

Decision Guide unification should remain deferred.

The certified Cross-City Comparison already handles mixed maturity. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette should remain `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` until a concrete customer, governance, or integration problem justifies migration.

Evidence Depth does not require full unification. A narrower transparency layer is preferable to mechanically relabeling or flattening editorial guides.
