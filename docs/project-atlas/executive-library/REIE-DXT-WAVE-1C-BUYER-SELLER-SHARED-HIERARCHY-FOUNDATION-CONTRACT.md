# REIE DXT Wave 1C Buyer Seller Shared Hierarchy Foundation Contract

Program: REIE Decision Experience Transformation (DXT 1.0)

Phase: Wave 1C - Buyer Seller Shared Hierarchy Foundation

Status: `BUYER_SELLER_SHARED_HIERARCHY_FOUNDATION_IMPLEMENTED`

Date: August 2, 2026

## 1. Contract Purpose

This contract defines the shared hierarchy foundation for future Buyer and Seller simplification work.

It is documentation and deterministic validation only. It does not create a shared React component, shared rendering abstraction, shared route, shared schema, runtime configuration, data model, API, CRM workflow, telemetry event, provider integration, or persistence layer.

## 2. Canonical Shared Hierarchy

Future Buyer and Seller page implementations must map visible sections to this canonical sequence:

1. page orientation;
2. governing decision question;
3. concise opening promise;
4. preparation themes;
5. tool or evidence continuation;
6. questions to verify;
7. professional and trust boundaries;
8. advisory transition;
9. compact next-decision continuations.

The sequence is directional, not a requirement to use identical copy or identical presentation. Buyer and Seller must remain content-specific.

## 3. Required Section Responsibilities

Page orientation:

- identify the journey without making the page feel like a product directory;
- state whether the customer is in a buyer or seller preparation context.

Governing decision question:

- frame the decision the customer is trying to make;
- avoid generic marketing claims;
- avoid scoring, qualification, valuation certainty, or suitability conclusions.

Concise opening promise:

- describe what the page helps organize;
- keep Search, financing, property, market, and advisory references at the right depth.

Preparation themes:

- group the customer work into a small number of decision themes;
- avoid dense educational article content above the governing question.

Tool or evidence continuation:

- route to existing destinations only;
- preserve existing Search, market, financing, property, home-worth, and contact routes;
- avoid hidden context transfer, provider activation, uploads, scheduling, CRM, telemetry, or persistence.

Questions to verify:

- name assumptions that require professional review;
- keep the page educational and preparatory rather than determinative.

Professional and trust boundaries:

- place limitations adjacent to the relevant decision area;
- preserve fair-housing, financing, valuation, evidence, legal, tax, insurance, title, HOA, zoning, permit, condition, and disclosure boundaries.

Advisory transition:

- appear after preparation context;
- invite focused advisor conversation without replacing professional review.

Compact next-decision continuations:

- offer a restrained set of next routes;
- avoid equal-weight CTA clusters and dense card walls.

## 4. Buyer-Specific Boundaries

Buyer content must preserve:

- readiness orientation;
- financing assumptions as user-entered and educational;
- Search continuation;
- property verification;
- offer and transaction preparation;
- Buyer Financing Planner boundaries;
- advisory transition.

Buyer content must not imply:

- qualification;
- approval;
- affordability conclusion;
- buying-power conclusion;
- lender recommendation;
- rate recommendation;
- loan recommendation;
- underwriting review;
- investment recommendation;
- demographic suitability;
- protected-class steering.

## 5. Seller-Specific Boundaries

Seller content must preserve:

- property preparation;
- known facts and evidence gaps;
- pricing-context boundaries;
- market exposure preparation;
- buyer-objection preparation;
- offer and transaction preparation;
- advisory transition.

Seller content must not imply:

- valuation certainty;
- price recommendation;
- guaranteed buyer behavior;
- guaranteed market outcome;
- seller net sheet;
- legal conclusion;
- title conclusion;
- condition conclusion;
- disclosure sufficiency;
- tax, insurance, HOA, zoning, permit, or environmental conclusion.

## 6. Shared CTA Contract

Future Buyer and Seller implementations must follow these CTA rules:

- one primary CTA per stage;
- at most one subordinate secondary CTA when necessary;
- no equal-weight CTA clusters;
- no scheduling, qualification, upload, provider, CRM, telemetry, or hidden context-transfer behavior;
- advisory appears after preparation;
- destinations must use existing routes;
- action labels must be verb-led and understandable.

## 7. Progressive Disclosure Contract

Future Buyer and Seller pages must not simultaneously show:

- all tools;
- all routes;
- dense disclosure walls;
- multiple primary CTAs;
- scoring or readiness labels;
- broad educational article content above the governing question;
- dashboard, portal, intake-product, or scorecard patterns.

Mobile behavior must read as a single decision narrative, not a compressed desktop layout.

## 8. Accessibility Contract

Future Buyer and Seller implementations must preserve:

- semantic heading order;
- logical reading order;
- keyboard-operable links;
- visible focus behavior;
- sufficient contrast;
- stable touch targets;
- no color-only meaning;
- no hidden essential guidance.

## 9. Protected Runtime Boundary

- route changes;
