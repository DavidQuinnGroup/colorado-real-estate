## 5. Current Customer And Business Gap

The largest current gap is not city coverage. It is the gap between the amount of certified intelligence now available and the clarity of the customer journey that turns that intelligence into action.

The repository now has strong point solutions:

- city intelligence
- search
- market context
- property intelligence
- neighborhood product
- buyer guidance
- seller guidance
- financing confidence education
- Grand Plan
- advisory/contact continuity

The remaining high-value problem is cross-surface experience quality: navigation consistency, route hierarchy, visual density, mobile polish, CTA clarity, city-to-search-to-property continuity, and the overall sense that REIE is one intelligent advisory experience rather than a collection of certified surfaces.

## 6. Initiatives Evaluated

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 Expansion

Potential scope: Niwot authority and search-support reconciliation.

Finding: valuable but no longer the best immediate move. Wave 4 would introduce an unincorporated/community authority question and search-support reconciliation after the public `FOUNDATION` backlog has already been closed. The incremental customer value is narrower than improving the whole customer journey.

### Boulder, Louisville, And Lafayette Unification

Potential scope: reconcile existing editorial Decision Guides with the governed `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` architecture.

Finding: useful but not urgent. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette are already certified as editorial Decision Guides, and their distinct maturity may be valuable. Unification could reduce duplication but risks flattening higher-quality editorial surfaces into a lower common standard unless a clear migration standard is authored first.

### Neighborhood/Submarket Intelligence

Potential scope: governed layer below city-level intelligence, including Gunbarrel and similar ambiguous entities.

Finding: strategically important but too risky as the immediate next implementation. Neighborhood/submarket intelligence has strong customer value and SEO authority potential, but it requires object-type governance, route conventions, fair-housing safeguards, evidence standards, neighborhood versus market-area classification, and specific treatment for ambiguous entities such as Gunbarrel.

### Cross-City Decision Comparison

Potential scope: neutral comparison across cities without rankings or suitability claims.

Finding: attractive and relatively reusable, but should follow experience integration. A comparison experience could use existing Decision Snapshots and continuity, but the risk of implicit ranking or suitability signaling is real. It is best planned after the site-wide decision journey is tightened.

### Evidence Depth And Data Integration

Potential scope: improve freshness, provenance, public-record integration, market-data depth, and source reliability.

Finding: high strategic leverage but dependency-heavy. Source-rights records show that repository-local MLS-derived city intelligence is approved, while government, assessor, permit, recorder, planning, imagery, and provider sources remain subject to legal/provider review. Evidence depth should proceed as a governed source-rights and provider-readiness program, not as the next public implementation by default.

### REIE Product Experience Next Cohesion Wave

Potential scope: broader product-experience work across homepage, search, city, market, property, neighborhood, buyer, seller, financing, Grand Plan, and advisory continuity.

Finding: highest priority. This initiative best converts completed intelligence into immediate customer and business value. It reuses certified foundations, avoids provider/schema/data dependencies, and addresses known open REIE 7.1 customer-experience requirements around navigation consistency, visual trust, mobile spacing, route clarity, cognitive load, and conversion continuity.

### Other Higher-Priority Repository Initiatives

The repository contains credible future candidates, including seller valuation route completion, buyer financing readiness, EPARB executive workspace, EOI source-quality readiness, source-rights activation readiness, GIS/provider governance, and local editorial/AEO work. None materially outranks the REIE product-experience next wave for immediate customer and conversion value under the current protected-boundary constraints.

## 13. Required Questions Answered

1. What has Local Decision Intelligence Phase 2 accomplished?

It created a reusable governed `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` city-intelligence architecture and certified Broomfield, Superior, Longmont, Denver, Erie, and Westminster, while preserving Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette editorial Decision Guides and protected boundaries.

2. What customer or business problem remains most important?

The most important problem is helping customers move through the now-expanded intelligence system with less friction, clearer next steps, stronger visual confidence, and better continuity from city context to search, property, buyer, seller, financing, Grand Plan, and advisory action.

3. Where is the largest gap between current capability and customer value?

The largest gap is experience integration. The platform has substantial certified intelligence, but the customer must still traverse multiple surfaces that can feel independently certified rather than one cohesive decision system.

4. Which initiative best uses the architecture already built?

The REIE Product Experience Next Cohesion Wave best uses the architecture already built because it integrates certified LDI, Decision Journey, Product Cohesion, Search, Property, Market, Neighborhood, Buyer, Seller, Financing, Grand Plan, and advisory surfaces without requiring new data infrastructure.

5. Which initiative creates the strongest near-term business leverage?

The product-experience wave creates the strongest near-term business leverage because it can improve conversion clarity and perceived product quality across all current traffic, not just one new city or one new content surface.

6. Which initiative carries the greatest avoidable risk?

Evidence depth and data integration carries the greatest avoidable risk because source-rights, provider confirmation, legal review, storage/display permissions, public-record semantics, and protected-boundary questions are not ready for activation.

7. Should Local Decision Intelligence expansion continue now, pause, or move to a slower governed cadence?

It should move to a slower governed cadence. Phase 2 closed the public backlog. The remaining candidates involve authority, search-support, object-type, or source-governance questions.

8. What should Codex implement next?

Codex should implement a bounded REIE Product Experience Next Cohesion Wave after explicit authorization.

9. What should Codex not implement yet?

Codex should not implement Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4, Niwot, neighborhood/submarket intelligence, Gunbarrel city-level intelligence, cross-city comparison, evidence/provider integration, Decision Guide unification, mortgage/lender features, AI, GIS, telemetry, search-ranking changes, APIs, schema changes, or production-mutating workflows yet.

10. What evidence would cause the priority decision to change?

The decision would change if executive strategy prioritizes a specific city lead-generation opportunity, search logs demonstrate high unmet demand for Niwot or another blocked city, source-rights/provider approvals unlock evidence depth safely, production analytics or customer feedback identifies a single higher-friction conversion blocker, or a compliance/business decision authorizes seller valuation or financing as the next highest-value route.
