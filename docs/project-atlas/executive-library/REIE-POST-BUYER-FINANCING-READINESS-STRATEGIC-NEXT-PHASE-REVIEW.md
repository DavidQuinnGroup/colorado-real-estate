## 5. Combined Buyer And Seller Readiness Accomplishments

The combined readiness programs created balanced, protected-boundary-safe preparation paths for both sides of the residential decision journey:

- sellers can organize preparation, documentation, verification, property-context questions, market-context questions, and advisory preparation through `/home-worth#seller-readiness`.
- buyers can organize financing documents, assumptions, lender questions, professional conversations, and next steps through `/buy#financing-readiness`.
- both programs preserve core journey continuity to Search, Market Context, Grand Plan, and Advisory.
- both programs remain non-persistent, non-scoring, non-automated, non-personalized, and non-predictive.
- both programs deliberately avoid the high-risk conclusions users may want next: value, price, sale timing, affordability, qualification, rates, approval, lender selection, and property-specific conclusions.

Strategic implication: REIE has now captured most of the near-term value available from educational readiness surfaces that do not depend on deeper evidence. The next material lift is not another generalized readiness layer; it is stronger governed evidence capability underneath existing city, comparison, property, neighborhood, seller, buyer, and advisory experiences.

## 6. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

The largest remaining gap is evidence depth and data integration.

Remaining customer and business problems:

- City, comparison, seller, buyer, property, and neighborhood experiences can orient users, but many deeper questions still depend on stronger source provenance, freshness, conflict handling, and qualified-source boundaries.
- Readiness experiences intentionally stop short of property-specific, valuation, qualification, affordability, permit, condition, title, insurance, environmental, municipal, HOA, or neighborhood conclusions.
- Advisory conversion can improve when the platform can surface what is known, what is stale, what is unresolved, what source rights allow, and what requires qualified review.
- Neighborhood and submarket intelligence remains strategically valuable but needs better object classification and evidence governance before broad public expansion.
- Property and seller evidence readiness has high value but would be premature without a broader source-rights and evidence-depth foundation.
- Decision Guide unification is not urgent because mixed-maturity comparison is certified and editorial guides remain safely governed.
- Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 remains an authority, classification, route, and search-support problem rather than the highest-value customer gap.

## 7. Initiatives Evaluated

### Evidence Depth And Data Integration

Finding: highest-priority next implementation candidate.

Evidence Depth is now the strongest enterprise-enabling next step. The repository already has source-rights, evidence-provenance, Geographic Intelligence, provider inventory, provider evaluation, and enterprise geographic consumer adapter foundations, but those foundations remain largely internal, fixture-backed, and activation-gated. A bounded next phase can create meaningful value by strengthening source-rights mapping, provenance metadata, freshness posture, conflict rules, and read-only evidence contracts without activating providers, public GIS, production writes, valuation, affordability, property conclusions, or customer-data flows.

Recommended first phase: contract-only or fixture-backed read-only evidence-depth foundation that audits and normalizes evidence posture for existing public intelligence surfaces.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: important, but should follow or be scoped inside the broader Evidence Depth foundation only if non-public and non-conclusive.

Property and seller evidence readiness would strengthen Seller Readiness, Home Worth, and Property Intelligence. It also carries the highest drift risk toward valuation, condition, title, insurance, inspection, legal, tax, or customer-document conclusions. It should not be implemented as a standalone public feature before evidence-depth governance defines source categories, rights, provenance, freshness, conflict handling, and customer-display boundaries.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture

Finding: architecture work is strategically important, but not the primary next initiative unless evidence depth is explicitly unavailable.

Neighborhood/submarket intelligence has high customer and SEO value. Current governance still needs object-type rules for neighborhoods, subdivisions, market areas, districts, and unincorporated communities; Gunbarrel-like ambiguity handling; route and registry governance; evidence requirements; fair-housing controls; and certification-scale planning. Architecture-only work is credible later, but evidence depth should come first because it supports neighborhood, property, city, and advisory layers.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: useful later, not necessary now.

Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette remain editorially certified. Cross-City Decision Comparison already preserves mixed `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` and `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` maturity without flattening editorial guides. Immediate unification would improve consistency but could reduce editorial depth and consume certification capacity without solving the larger evidence gap.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

The public `FOUNDATION` backlog is complete. Niwot still requires authority, unincorporated-community classification, relationship to Boulder and Longmont, route, registry, and search-support reconciliation. It should be resolved through geographic or neighborhood/submarket governance, not automatic city-level expansion.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: no broad follow-up justified now.

Product Experience, Cross-City Comparison, Seller Readiness, and Buyer Financing Readiness have recently improved journey continuity. Targeted fixes remain appropriate if production evidence identifies a specific friction point, but no current repository record shows a broad product-experience wave outranks evidence depth.

### Advisory Conversion And Human-Handoff Readiness

Finding: valuable later, not primary now.

Advisory handoff can improve conversion and lead quality if it remains presentational and non-persistent. It should not precede evidence depth because better handoff quality depends on clearer evidence, limitation, freshness, and qualified-source framing. CRM tasks, automation, forms, tracking, lead scoring, alerts, queues, email, or customer-data workflows remain unauthorized.

### Other Repository-Supported Priorities

The repository contains credible longer-term programs across source-rights readiness, Geographic Intelligence, Decision Guide unification, neighborhood/submarket architecture, seller/property evidence readiness, Search/map polish, public-trust disclosure review, and customer-experience visual polish. None materially outranks a bounded Evidence Depth and Data Integration phase after buyer and seller readiness are certified.

## 11. Evidence-Depth Finding

Evidence Depth is now the strongest next candidate.

Rationale:

- Buyer and seller readiness have reached the practical limit of education-only protected-boundary value.
- City, comparison, property, neighborhood, buyer, seller, financing, and advisory surfaces all benefit from clearer source posture.
- Source-rights records show MLS-derived city intelligence is approved, while assessor, recorder, permit, planning, imagery, and provider sources remain gated by legal/provider review.
- GIS Sprint 2 already defines provider-neutral evidence identity, source identity, immutable versions, provenance chains, freshness, licensing, permitted use, conflict preservation, and fail-closed behavior.
- EKCP and Geographic Intelligence records provide internal consumption and object-governance patterns but keep customer activation unauthorized.
- A bounded phase can proceed without provider activation by staying contract-only, fixture-backed, and repository-local.

Evidence Depth should not begin with live acquisition. It should begin by making the existing evidence posture explicit, testable, and reusable.

## 12. Property / Seller Evidence Finding

Property / Seller Evidence Readiness should follow Evidence Depth or be included only as a non-public, non-conclusive evidence-category use case.

It should not be a standalone public implementation yet because it could drift into:

- valuation
- condition conclusions
- title, insurance, legal, tax, inspection, structural, environmental, lending, or appraisal conclusions
- ownership lookup
- public-record acquisition
- document upload or customer-data persistence
- provider dependency

## 13. Neighborhood / Submarket Finding

Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence is ready for further architecture work after or alongside evidence-depth governance, but not for public route expansion now.

Needed prerequisites:

- object-type standards for neighborhood, subdivision, market area, district, and unincorporated community.
- Gunbarrel-like ambiguity policy.
- route and registry governance.
- source-rights and evidence posture per object type.
- fair-housing and steering validation at scale.
- certification model for many future neighborhood objects.

## 14. Decision Guide Unification Finding

Decision Guide unification remains unnecessary now.

Mixed-maturity comparison is certified and safe. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette can remain `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` while Broomfield, Superior, Longmont, Denver, Erie, and Westminster remain `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`. Unification may reduce future complexity, but it should not precede evidence depth unless a future comparison, source-rights, or certification record proves mixed maturity is creating material customer or operational friction.

## 15. Local Decision Intelligence Cadence

Local Decision Intelligence expansion should remain paused and slower-governed.

Niwot should not be activated now. Niwot authority, unincorporated-community status, object classification, route behavior, relationship to Boulder and Longmont, and search-support reconciliation should be handled through later geographic or neighborhood/submarket governance rather than automatic city expansion.

## 16. Product Experience Finding

The recent sequence certified homepage/buy/sell/home-worth/grand-plan/contact cohesion, Cross-City Decision Comparison, Seller Readiness, and Buyer Financing Readiness. Product Experience follow-up should be targeted only if new production evidence shows a specific discovery, CTA, mobile, or handoff friction.
