## 5. Public Trust And Evidence-Limitation Completeness Finding

Finding: the current public trust and evidence-limitation layer is sufficiently complete for the certified Decision Guide surface.

The remaining customer-value gap is no longer guide-level evidence transparency. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette now explain how to read citywide context, evidence limits, recency, unresolved uncertainty, source-use boundaries, property-specific limits, professional review, and decision boundaries without exposing internal metadata or weakening editorial depth.

Further work on evidence transparency would be lower value unless attached to a concrete new domain such as Neighborhood / Submarket readiness or Property / Seller Evidence Readiness.

## 6. Decision Guide Unification Finding

Finding: Decision Guide unification remains unnecessary.

Evidence Transparency solved the material customer-trust problem that full unification would have tried to address, while preserving Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette editorial depth. Mixed-maturity handling remains certified in Cross-City Comparison, and the editorial guides now carry public limitation-forward context. Mechanical migration to `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` would create regression and flattening risk without a clear customer-value gain.

Do not relabel Boulder, Louisville, or Lafayette.

## 7. Diminishing-Returns Finding

Finding: another standalone internal foundation or generic evidence-only phase would create diminishing returns.

REIE now has certified public guidance, comparison, readiness, handoff, Decision Guide transparency, Evidence Depth, Controlled Evidence, Advisory Operating, Geographic Intelligence, and Neighborhood / Submarket architecture foundations. The next highest-value work should convert existing foundations into governed customer and local-authority value through a bounded implementation wave, not add another abstract foundation.

## 8. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

Largest remaining customer-value gap: governed sub-city intelligence.

The public product now explains city-level and editorial-guide limits well, but customers still lack a governed bridge from citywide context to neighborhood, subdivision, corridor, market-area, and unincorporated-community context. Existing neighborhood routes and records show useful local context, but the certified Neighborhood / Submarket architecture has not yet been applied to a bounded implementation wave.

Material gaps:

- existing neighborhood routes are public but not yet reconciled with the new object-type, maturity, evidence, ambiguity, and readiness architecture
- route, registry, Search, map, and boundary readiness remain separate and fail closed
- Gunbarrel and Niwot remain blocked and unresolved
- public new-route creation remains premature
- Property / Seller Evidence Readiness has business value but remains closer to privacy, property identity, ownership, public-record, valuation, condition, and professional-boundary risk
- Product Experience work lacks concrete production evidence that it outranks geographic/local-authority depth

## 9. Initiatives Evaluated

### First Governed Neighborhood / Submarket Implementation Wave

Finding: strongest next implementation priority.

The certified architecture is ready for one tightly bounded wave that applies object-type, identity, evidence, source-rights, maturity, limitation, fair-housing, and activation-blocker governance to existing neighborhood/submarket surfaces without creating new public routes or changing route eligibility.

Recommended first wave: internal readiness plus existing-route preservation only.

The wave should evaluate and govern existing neighborhood route candidates, preserve current route identities, add internal readiness and limitation contracts, and create deterministic checks that prove no route, registry, Search, map, Niwot, Gunbarrel, provider, API, schema, persistence, or production behavior was activated.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: strong secondary priority, but not the immediate next implementation.

Property / Seller Evidence Readiness can create business and advisory value later as an internal, fixture-backed, non-customer-specific evidence-category foundation. It should support Seller Readiness, Home Worth, Property Intelligence, and Advisory preparation without address lookup, ownership lookup, uploads, providers, valuation, condition conclusions, public records, or customer-specific analysis.

It remains riskier than Neighborhood / Submarket implementation because it approaches property identity, title, permits, disclosure, insurance, structural and environmental review, HOA and municipal materials, privacy, ownership, valuation, and professional boundaries.

### Niwot Governance Reconciliation

Finding: useful later, but not the next primary implementation.

Neighborhood / Submarket Architecture provides the vocabulary required for Niwot reconciliation, but repository records still classify Niwot as blocked and unresolved. Niwot should not become a city-level Decision Guide, public route, registry entry, Search behavior, map support, or LDI Wave 4 activation by momentum.

A future governance-only Niwot phase may determine whether Niwot belongs as an unincorporated-community object, census-place object, neighborhood/submarket object, market-context object, or remains unresolved.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

Wave 4 should not proceed until object-type certainty, Search support, route readiness, evidence readiness, source rights, and Neighborhood / Submarket overlap are resolved for candidates such as Niwot, Brighton, Firestone, Frederick, Thornton, or later governed communities.

City expansion is currently lower value than applying certified sub-city governance to existing local surfaces.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: remain deferred.

Evidence Transparency improved customer understanding without forcing migration. No repository evidence shows a current customer problem that requires unifying Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette with `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`.

### Embedded Evidence Integration

Finding: embed evidence integration inside the selected Neighborhood / Submarket wave.

The next wave should reuse Evidence Depth and source-rights concepts for internal readiness, limitation, and non-exposure checks. It should not become a standalone generic evidence foundation.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: not justified by current production evidence.

No repository evidence currently shows that discovery, CTA clarity, mobile friction, duplicate pathways, cognitive load, or conversion continuity issues outrank neighborhood/submarket depth. Product Experience, Decision Journey, Cross-City Comparison, readiness, Advisory Handoff, and Decision Guide transparency are certified.

### Other Repository-Supported Priority

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks a first governed Neighborhood / Submarket implementation wave.
