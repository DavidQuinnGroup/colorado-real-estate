## 1. Executive Lesson

Enterprise quality is the bridge between internal knowledge and future customer trust.

Sprint 1 proved memory. Sprint 2 proved retrieval. Sprint 3 proved that PROJECT ATLAS can judge governed knowledge before any public experience depends on it.

---

## 2. What Worked

- The Sprint 2 read model provided enough stable metadata to drive quality evaluation.
- A reusable quality contract could be implemented without binding to Prisma, Supabase, or geographic-specific persistence.
- Required quality scenarios could be demonstrated without adding production geography.
- Customer-visible quality scores could be explicitly prohibited in code and validation.
- Activation readiness could distinguish internal readiness from customer activation.

---

## 3. What To Preserve

Future implementation sprints should preserve:

- quality evaluation as an internal enterprise capability;
- reusable domain-neutral contracts;
- explicit customer-activation denial by default;
- runtime import scanning;
- deterministic validation fixtures;
- quality recommendations that help humans decide what remains blocked, watchlisted, or ready.

---

## 4. What To Improve

Sprint 4 should convert quality assessments into a practical internal readiness ledger.

Recommended improvements:

- summarize which knowledge is blocked, watchlisted, or internally ready;
- preserve reasons and evidence for each readiness result;
- keep quality and activation separate;
- avoid customer-facing score language;
- prepare later internal operator review without introducing runtime activation.

---

## 5. Strategic Implication

PROJECT ATLAS now has the first internal quality layer for governed knowledge.

The next strategic move is controlled readiness management: not more architecture, and not customer activation, but a disciplined internal ledger that can help decide which knowledge might eventually deserve activation review.
