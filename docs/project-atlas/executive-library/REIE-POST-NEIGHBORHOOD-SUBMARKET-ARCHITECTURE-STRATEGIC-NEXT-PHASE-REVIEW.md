## 5. Geographic Foundation Completeness Finding

Finding: the geographic-object foundation is complete enough for bounded planning, evidence transparency, and internal readiness work, but not enough by itself to justify public neighborhood or submarket activation.

The architecture resolved the object-governance layer that previously blocked future sub-city intelligence. It did not resolve route design, registry activation, Search support, map rights, public content standards, responsive public UI, or production certification for any specific public neighborhood/submarket implementation.

## 6. Public Implementation Readiness Finding

Finding: public neighborhood implementation remains premature.

Reasons:

- architecture readiness explicitly creates no route
- registry readiness creates no public registry entry
- Search support remains distinct from route and registry readiness
- boundary posture remains distinct from identity and route readiness
- Gunbarrel and Niwot remain blocked and unresolved
- route, registry, Search, map, content, rights, fair-housing, responsive, deterministic, and production certification prerequisites still require separate authorization

The safest first neighborhood/submarket implementation wave would be internal or route-preservation oriented, not new public-route creation.

## 7. Diminishing-Returns Finding

Finding: another standalone internal architecture phase would create diminishing returns unless attached to a concrete customer-facing or domain-specific need.

REIE has now certified public guidance, readiness, advisory handoff, evidence foundations, advisory operating standards, and geographic-object architecture. The next highest-value work should convert those foundations into bounded customer trust and authority value, not add another abstract foundation without a product target.

## 8. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

The largest remaining customer and business gap is not the absence of another foundation. It is the lack of clear, public, limitation-forward evidence transparency on the existing editorial Decision Guides.

Material gaps:

- Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette are `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED`, but the public customer does not yet get a consistently governed explanation of evidence limitations, freshness posture, source-category framing, citywide versus property-specific boundaries, or qualified-source reminders.
- Cross-City Comparison already exposes mixed maturity as governance and evidence structure, not a grade, but the underlying Decision Guides can carry that trust posture more clearly.
- Evidence Depth and Controlled Evidence Integration are certified but remain non-public; the next useful step is a public-copy-only transparency layer that uses safe concepts without exposing internal metadata.
- Neighborhood/Submarket public activation is still gated by route, registry, Search, map, rights, content, and certification prerequisites.
- Property/Seller Evidence Readiness remains useful but has higher privacy, ownership, provider, public-record, valuation, condition, and professional-boundary risk.

## 9. Initiatives Evaluated

### Decision Guide Evidence Transparency

Finding: strongest next implementation priority.

A bounded public-copy-only transparency layer can improve customer trust, authority, mixed-maturity clarity, and evidence-aware decision quality across Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette without creating public evidence labels, internal metadata exposure, provider activation, APIs, schema changes, new routes, or dynamic evidence conclusions.

The safe first scope should include visible limitation-forward copy, source-category framing, freshness or recency framing, citywide-versus-property-specific boundaries, qualified-source reminders, editorial maturity explanation, unavailable or unresolved evidence disclosure, and consistency with Cross-City Comparison.

### First Governed Neighborhood / Submarket Implementation Wave

Finding: high-value secondary priority, but public implementation remains premature.

The architecture is sufficient for an internal readiness or route-preservation wave. It is not sufficient to create new public neighborhood/submarket routes. The safest first object types are existing governed neighborhood contexts and non-activated candidate records that can be evaluated without altering public eligibility.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: useful later and now better supported by evidence and geographic architecture, but still not the next primary initiative.

It should remain internal, contract-level, fixture-backed, and non-customer-specific. It should not activate address lookup, ownership lookup, uploads, public records, providers, valuation, appraisal substitution, condition conclusions, or customer-specific analysis.

### Niwot / Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 Governance

Finding: Niwot governance posture improved because the architecture now provides object-type and relationship vocabulary, but Niwot remains blocked from activation.

Niwot likely belongs in unincorporated-community, census-place, community, or neighborhood/submarket governance before any city-level Local Decision Intelligence expansion. A governance-only reconciliation phase may be useful later, but not ahead of Decision Guide evidence transparency.

Gunbarrel remains blocked and should be used only as a governance case until classification, parentage, boundaries, route, registry, Search, rights, and fair-housing posture are resolved.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: full unification remains deferred.

Evidence transparency is more valuable than full unification because it preserves Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette editorial depth while addressing the current trust and limitation gap. Mechanical migration to `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` could flatten editorial value and create unnecessary regression risk.

### Embedded Domain-Specific Evidence Integration

Finding: evidence integration should be embedded inside Decision Guide transparency, Neighborhood/Submarket readiness, Property/Seller evidence, or Local Decision Intelligence source posture, not run as another generic foundation.

For the selected Decision Guide transparency initiative, the integration should remain public-copy-only, deterministic, non-dynamic, and non-metadata-exposing.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: not justified by current repository evidence.

No current production evidence proves that a targeted product-experience change outranks evidence transparency or geographic depth work. Product Experience, Comparison, Seller Readiness, Buyer Financing Readiness, Advisory Handoff, Advisory Operating, and public route safety are certified.

### Other Repository-Supported Priority

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks Decision Guide Evidence Transparency after Neighborhood / Submarket Architecture closure.

## 13. Decision Guide Evidence Transparency Finding

Decision Guide Evidence Transparency should come next.

Reasons:

- Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette already share a Decision Guide platform and validation contract.
- The platform already has public trust boundaries and customer-facing verification sections.
- Cross-City Comparison already relies on mixed-maturity framing and would benefit from aligned guide-level transparency.
- Evidence Depth is certified but internal; the next safe public use is limitation-forward copy, not metadata exposure.
- The work can be bounded to public copy and deterministic validation without providers, data acquisition, APIs, schema changes, public evidence labels, or dynamic evaluator output.
- Customer value is immediate because the guides become clearer about what REIE knows, what remains limited, and where property-specific or qualified-source review is needed.

The implementation must not expose evidence IDs, source-rights enums, support levels, conflict codes, provenance chains, fixture outputs, confidence scores, provider data, internal summaries, or automated conclusions.

## 14. First Neighborhood / Submarket Wave Finding

A first Neighborhood/Submarket wave is not the next primary initiative.

The architecture is now sufficient for bounded internal readiness work or existing-route preservation, but public neighborhood implementation is still premature because specific objects have not been authorized for route or registry activation and Search/map/source-rights/content certification remains separate.

The next wave should not create public routes. If later authorized, the safest scope would be internal candidate readiness, existing route preservation, or evidence/content readiness assessment using already-certified object distinctions.

## 20. Decision Guide Unification Finding

Decision Guide Evidence Transparency is more valuable than full Decision Guide unification.

Evidence transparency addresses the actual customer trust gap while preserving Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette editorial depth. Full unification could create migration risk, flatten editorial value, and reopen certified guide behavior without proving added customer value.

Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette should remain `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` unless a separate review proves customer confusion, governance drag, or integration necessity.
