## 2. Product Vision Assessment

The governing product vision from Product Excellence Roadmap 1.0 was that a Colorado buyer or seller should feel within five minutes that David Quinn Group operates with unusual clarity, intelligence, taste, discipline, and local authority.

Current assessment:

- Clarity: high. The public navigation and footer now give customers a stable product map.
- Intelligence: high. Search, property decision support, market intelligence, neighborhood context, seller confidence, and buyer confidence are visible.
- Taste: high, with targeted remaining polish opportunities. The product is restrained, dark, premium, and editorial rather than generic.
- Discipline: high. The site repeatedly states what it can and cannot claim, especially around valuation, affordability, AI, GIS, provider, and source boundaries.
- Local authority: high. Market, city, neighborhood, and property contexts create stronger Colorado specificity than a generic brokerage site.

Does REIE feel like one product?

Yes. The certified public navigation, Home Worth route, Buyer Confidence framework, market context, and property decision surfaces now create a product with shared language and shared route logic. The product is not simply a search page with extra pages around it. It now feels like a guided real estate intelligence system.

Where does the customer lose confidence?

The highest-confidence loss now occurs around financing. Buyer Confidence correctly acknowledges affordability assumptions, but there is no governed financing destination, no Mortgage Calculator, and no lender-boundary experience. That absence is preferable to unsafe financial claims, but it remains the most visible unfinished customer expectation.

Where is navigation strongest?

Navigation is strongest in the global header/footer and major public route set: Search, Market, Home Worth, Sell, Grand Plan, About, and Contact. It is also strong between market pages, property pages, and search due to certified contextual links.

Where is navigation weakest?

Navigation is weakest around unresolved future experiences: mortgage, lender/referral posture, Sundance/editorial authority, and AEO-driven content paths. These are not broken routes; they are product areas that remain intentionally absent or deferred.

Where is educational flow strongest?

Educational flow is strongest in Seller Confidence and Buyer Confidence. `/home-worth` explains uncertainty before action. The buyer journey now frames known facts, comparison, verification, questions, and next steps.

Where is educational flow weakest?

Educational flow is weakest in financing and long-form editorial authority. The property page includes financial questions, and search now includes affordability awareness, but REIE does not yet provide a complete buyer-financing education path.

What parts still feel fragmented?

- financing expectations across search/property/affordability language.
- article/editorial authority relative to the main product.
- Grand Plan as a potentially powerful planning concept that is present but not yet the flagship customer experience.
- map visual delight and advanced geographic intelligence remain bounded by provider/GIS authorization.

What creates the most trust?

- no instant valuation posture.
- affordability-awareness boundaries.
- Property Decision Briefs.
- market and neighborhood context with source boundaries.
- consistent public navigation and disclosures.
- premium restraint rather than aggressive lead capture.

What creates the greatest competitive advantage?

The combination of property decision support, market intelligence, neighborhood intelligence, seller valuation education, and buyer confidence sequencing creates the strongest differentiation. National portals can provide listings; REIE now provides disciplined local interpretation.

## 3. Customer Journey Assessment

### Seller Journey

Beginning:

- the seller can enter through Home Worth, Sell, Market, Footer, or property-related seller links.

Middle:

- `/home-worth` explains why value is difficult, what affects value, why automated estimates differ, and why local expertise matters.
- `/sell` supports deeper strategy and preparation.

End:

- the customer can request a professional review while understanding that no instant valuation, automated estimate, or brokerage relationship is created by reading the page.

Confidence gained:

- sellers understand that pricing is a human, market-aware, preparation-aware conversation.

Confidence lost:

- low. The route is certified and clear. The remaining opportunity is deeper seller-specific market evidence, not basic confidence.

### Buyer Journey

Beginning:

- the buyer enters through home, search, featured communities, market pages, or property pages.

Middle:

- search supports discovery and refinement.
- property pages support known facts, comparison, verification, and questions.
- market and neighborhood pages explain context.

End:

- the buyer can continue searching, open market context, ask a question, or schedule a tour.

Confidence gained:

- buyers now understand what to know, compare, verify, ask, and do next.

Confidence lost:

- financing confidence remains incomplete. REIE tells buyers which assumptions to verify but does not yet provide a complete financing education destination.

### Explorer Journey

Beginning:

- the explorer starts at home or market.

Middle:

- the explorer can browse communities, market context, neighborhoods, and search inventory.

End:

- the explorer can enter search, contact, seller review, or Grand Plan.

Confidence gained:

- the product feels locally authoritative and not overly transactional.

Confidence lost:

- editorial depth could be stronger for lifestyle, relocation, and cultural discovery.

### Researcher Journey

Beginning:

- the researcher starts with market, neighborhood, property, or article content.

Middle:

- market and property pages provide source-bound decision context.

End:

- the researcher can ask focused questions or continue to related areas.

Confidence gained:

- REIE is stronger than typical brokerage content because it connects property, market, neighborhood, and verification questions.

Confidence lost:

- no broad editorial library or AEO-ready knowledge hub is yet certified as a product layer.

### Returning Visitor

Beginning:

- the returning visitor can re-enter through stable navigation and familiar route names.

Middle:

- the visitor can move between Search, Market, Home Worth, Sell, and Contact without relearning the product.

End:

- inquiry, seller review, contact, or continued research.

Confidence gained:

- stable navigation and repeated confidence language make the product feel maintained.

Confidence lost:

- saved-search and personalization exist as bounded capabilities but are not yet a visible returning-visitor experience.

### Repeat Visitor

Beginning:

- repeat visitors may come back to compare new inventory, track a market, or revisit a property.

Middle:

- current product supports this manually through search, property, and market routes.

End:

- repeat visitors can contact or continue research.

Confidence gained:

- context remains understandable.

Confidence lost:

- no activated measurement/personalization loop or customer account layer exists, by design.

## 8. Recommended Next Initiative

Recommend exactly one next initiative:

`BUYER_FINANCING_AND_AFFORDABILITY_CONFIDENCE_READINESS_REVIEW`

Executive objective:

Determine how REIE should address buyer financing expectations without unsafe calculator claims, lender endorsement risk, financial advice, new persistence, or regulatory ambiguity.

Why this is the highest-return investment:

- Buyer Confidence is now certified, and its most visible open thread is affordability.
- The REIE requirements register still identifies Mortgage Calculator and recommended lender experience as unresolved customer requests.
- Search and property pages already contain affordability-awareness and financial-question posture, which creates architectural reuse.
- A readiness review can turn an obvious customer expectation into a governed product strategy before any implementation.
- Financing confidence has high customer value and high lead-generation value, but also high compliance risk. That makes a readiness review more valuable than immediate implementation.

Recommended review scope:

- Mortgage Calculator product posture.
- lender/referral boundary.
- affordability assumptions.
- disclosure requirements.
- customer-data and persistence boundaries.
- relationship to property financial questions.
- relationship to inquiry and consultation.
- compliance and legal review requirements.
- whether implementation should be educational, calculator-based, lender-referral-based, or deferred.

Explicit exclusions:

- no calculator implementation.
- no lender page implementation.
- no lender recommendation.
- no financing application.
- no pre-approval workflow.
- no database schema change.
- no AI guidance.
- no telemetry activation.
- no provider activation.
