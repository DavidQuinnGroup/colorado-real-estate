Runtime authorization: `false`

## Responsibility Ownership

Homepage owns orientation and journey entry.

Search owns active criteria, current inventory, comparison context, map/list evidence, and transition to Property.

Property owns address-level evaluation, verification, Property Inquiry, and property-specific preparation.

Buyer owns preparation to buy, evidence, assumptions, financing-readiness education, and professional-conversation preparation.

Seller owns preparation for market exposure, condition and pricing-context assumptions, evidence gaps, and professional-conversation preparation.

Market owns broad market briefing and investigation pathways.

City Market owns city-level market evidence and Neighborhood/Search handoff.

Neighborhood owns neutral place orientation and transition to inventory or Property.

Advisory owns preparation for a focused professional conversation.

Contact owns initiation of a general conversation and route-choice clarity.

Grand Plan owns broader decision-framework orientation.

Home Worth owns Seller readiness and context, not valuation certainty.

Compare owns structured comparison without recommendation or suitability scoring.

## Governing-Question Principles

Every route must answer the decision it owns. Professional-preparation questions should help customers organize what they know, what remains unverified, what assumptions they are carrying, and which pathway fits the question.

Route-specific questions may differ. Exact copy uniformity is not required.

## Evidence-Category Principles

Use route-appropriate variants of:

- `Evidence available now`
- `Evidence reviewed or available`
- `Evidence still needing verification`
- `Evidence still needed`
- `Evidence not available from this route`

These labels must describe public route evidence only. They must not imply that the customer personally reviewed private information or that REIE verified facts outside the route.

## Assumption Treatment

Assumptions must be separated from available evidence. Buyer assumptions may involve financing and property-review readiness. Seller assumptions may involve condition and pricing context. Property assumptions may involve listing facts, condition, records, and transaction details. Market and Neighborhood assumptions remain evidence-gathering context only.

## Unknown Treatment

Unknowns must be limitation-forward and framed without false certainty. Acceptable route-specific terms include `Unknown`, `Unknowns`, `Unknown from current evidence`, `Not confirmed here`, and equivalent customer-readable phrasing.

## Verification Treatment

Verification must remain source-specific and professional-review aware. Routes may identify source, Property, lender, inspection, title, HOA, insurance, appraisal, legal, tax, contract, market, Seller Review, Property Inquiry, Advisory, or Contact review needs. Routes must not answer protected professional questions.

## Question Treatment

Questions may be organized, grouped, and carried forward. Questions must not become professional answers, recommendations, predictions, scores, rankings, suitability conclusions, or inferred customer intent.

## Conversation-Priority Treatment

Conversation priorities must remain static, visible, route-owned, non-persistent, non-scoring, and non-prescriptive. No saved priority, hidden state, URL parameter, form prefill, CRM classification, lead score, telemetry event, customer profile, automatic routing, or inferred prioritization is authorized.

## Pathway Ownership

Property Inquiry is the specialized path for Property-specific questions and existing certified submissions.

Advisory is preparation before a focused professional conversation.

Contact begins a general conversation and route choice.

Buyer and Seller own their respective preparation contexts.

Search, Market, City Market, Neighborhood, Grand Plan, Home Worth, and Compare support evidence gathering, orientation, and next-route choice without taking over professional preparation.

## Advisory-Versus-Contact Distinction

Advisory prepares. Contact begins.

Advisory may organize evidence, assumptions, unknowns, questions, conversation priorities, and public limitations. Contact may help customers choose a path and begin a general conversation. Neither route may create hidden transfer, representation, a professional answer, a new form, CRM work, email, scheduling, persistence, or telemetry without separate authorization.

## Direct-Entry Requirements

Every route must remain understandable when opened directly, refreshed, copied, externally referred, or opened at an anchor. No prior journey state, hidden profile, URL-context expansion, localStorage, cookies, or telemetry-derived context may be required.

## Canonical Requirements

Canonical URLs must remain clean route URLs. Anchor entry must not alter canonical ownership. Search parameters may support existing Search behavior but must not become hidden professional-preparation transfer.

## Privacy And Consent Requirements

Use visible public context only. No identity, email, phone, private notes, saved searches, saved Properties, planner inputs, estimator inputs, financial assumptions, credit information, protected characteristics, CRM status, lead score, cookies, localStorage, telemetry-derived context, unsubmitted form content, or confidential information may transfer across routes.

No prechecked consent, implied marketing consent, automatic representation, or response-time promise is authorized.

## Representation Boundaries

Professional preparation, path selection, evidence review, or inquiry orientation does not create representation, fiduciary duties, brokerage relationship, legal advice, tax advice, lending advice, valuation advice, investment advice, suitability conclusions, or AI professional conclusions.

## Financial And Lending Boundaries

Routes must not provide mortgage approval, qualification, affordability, buying power, underwriting, credit readiness, lender ranking, lender recommendation, rate prediction, or lending advice. Buyer Financing Planner and financing education remain educational and preserved.

## Valuation And Pricing Boundaries

Routes must not provide appraisal equivalence, valuation certainty, verified property value, list-price recommendation, sale-price prediction, pricing strategy, market-timing recommendation, buyer-demand prediction, or outcome promise. Home Worth and Home Value Estimator remain context-setting only.

## Legal And Tax Boundaries

Routes may identify legal and tax questions for review. Routes must not answer them, recommend legal or tax positions, or imply professional review has occurred.

## Investment And Suitability Boundaries

Routes must not claim investment fit, appreciation, return, suitability, best choice, best neighborhood, or personalized recommendation.

## Fair-Housing Boundaries

Routes must not introduce protected-class steering, demographic suitability, family-status assumptions, neighborhood fit, neighborhood rankings, best-neighborhood claims, safety guarantees, unsupported crime conclusions, school-quality conclusions, or personalized location recommendations based on protected or inferred traits.

## Professional-Limit Placement

Professional limits must appear near reliance points: when a route presents evidence, verification needs, questions, pathways, or next actions. Boundary language should be concise and customer-readable.

## CTA Hierarchy Principles

Dominant actions must match route responsibility:

- Search: compare/refine/open Property.
- Property: Property Inquiry first, Advisory second, Contact third.
- Buyer: buyer preparation, Search/Property, Advisory, Contact.
- Seller: Seller Review first, Home Worth/Market/Search, Advisory, Contact.
- Advisory: Begin a focused conversation after preparation.
- Contact: choose the starting point.
- Market/City/Neighborhood: continue research, Search, Property, or Advisory.

Subordinate actions must remain compact and clearly secondary.

## Responsive And Accessibility Principles

Preparation surfaces should remain concise, scannable, keyboard focusable, readable at mobile/tablet/desktop widths, and free of document-level horizontal overflow. Route-specific density is acceptable when the route is evidence-heavy, but first-viewport overload should not be created by professional-preparation copy.

## Route-Specific-Language Exceptions

Buyer may use financing-readiness language, but not lending conclusions.

Seller may use condition and pricing-context language, but not valuation or pricing recommendations.

Property may use address-level and Property Inquiry language.

Advisory may use preparation and focused-conversation language.

Contact may use route-choice and general-conversation language.

Search, Market, City Market, Neighborhood, Grand Plan, Home Worth, and Compare may use evidence-gathering, orientation, and comparison language.
