## 6. Remaining Customer And Business Gaps

The largest remaining gap is internal operating consistency: REIE now has certified public guidance, readiness, handoff, and internal evidence-posture mechanics, but no governed internal standard for how human advisors should consistently use those capabilities without overstating conclusions.

Remaining gaps:

- Advisors need a non-customer-specific conversation framework for using Comparison, Buyer, Financing, Seller, Grand Plan, Advisory Handoff, and evidence-limit concepts consistently.
- Evidence-aware preparation prompts exist internally, but they are not yet organized into an advisor operating standard.
- Professional escalation boundaries need a consistent internal checklist so legal, tax, lending, appraisal, inspection, engineering, insurance, title, environmental, municipal, HOA, valuation, pricing, affordability, and qualification topics stay with qualified professionals.
- Neighborhood/submarket authority remains valuable but unresolved at the object-governance layer.
- Property/seller evidence readiness remains constrained by property identity, ownership/privacy risk, public-record/provider dependencies, and valuation or condition-conclusion risk.
- Decision Guide unification remains optional because mixed-maturity comparison is already governed and certified.
- Product Experience follow-up lacks specific production evidence of friction.

## 7. Initiatives Evaluated

### Advisory Operating Readiness

Finding: highest-value next implementation candidate.

Advisory Operating Readiness would create an internal, non-customer-specific operating standard for consistent human use of REIE. It can reuse Advisory Handoff, Controlled Evidence Integration, Evidence Depth limitations, Cross-City Comparison, Buyer Financing Readiness, Seller Readiness, Grand Plan, Contact, and professional-boundary governance.

Safe first-version scope should be documentation, contract, checklist, fixture, or deterministic validation only. It should not create CRM workflows, customer records, lead scoring, lead routing, tracking, telemetry, automated outreach, saved customer preparation, transaction management, or advisor performance scoring.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture

Finding: strongest long-term secondary priority, not the next implementation.

Neighborhood and submarket intelligence has high customer, SEO, local-authority, and enterprise leverage. It is ready for architecture review, but not stronger than Advisory Operating Readiness now because it still requires object-type taxonomy, canonical identity, parent/child geography, neighborhood/subdivision/district/corridor/market-area/unincorporated-community policy, Gunbarrel and Niwot classification handling, route and registry governance, evidence requirements, source-rights integration, fair-housing controls, and scalable certification.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: valuable but not primary now.

The certified Evidence Depth and Controlled Evidence Integration work makes property/seller evidence categories more feasible, but the risk surface remains higher than Advisory Operating Readiness. Property identity, ownership, privacy, title, condition, disclosure, insurance, structural, environmental, municipal, HOA, valuation, and professional-boundary issues can drift into customer-specific or professional conclusions. A future phase should remain internal, fixture-backed, and conclusion-free.

### Second Controlled Evidence Integration

Finding: not necessary as the next standalone phase.

The first controlled integration sufficiently proved the foundation for operating use. A second integration could be useful if tied to a higher-value domain, such as neighborhood architecture readiness, property/seller evidence categories, Decision Guide transparency, or Local Decision Intelligence source posture. As a standalone repeat, it risks duplicating proof already obtained.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: not the next priority.

Evidence architecture increases future transparency options but does not create an immediate need to reconcile Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette with `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`. Mixed-maturity comparison remains governed and certified. Editorial depth should be preserved until repository evidence shows customer confusion, governance cost, or evidence integration requirements that justify migration.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

Niwot and similar unincorporated-community questions should not be treated as simple city expansion. They are better resolved through Neighborhood / Submarket or broader geographic-object architecture because authority, object type, parent/child relationship, route, registry, and search-support questions remain unresolved.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: not justified without new production evidence.

The public product layer recently certified cohesion, comparison, seller readiness, buyer financing readiness, and advisory handoff. A small product change may be appropriate later if production evidence shows specific discoverability, CTA, mobile, duplicate-path, or cognitive-load friction. A broad cohesion wave would be redundant.

### Other Repository-Supported Priority

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks Advisory Operating Readiness after Controlled Evidence Integration closure.

## 24. Required Questions

1. What did Controlled Evidence Depth Integration accomplish?

It proved that certified Evidence Depth posture can be reused in internal Advisory preparation to generate bounded question, limitation, verification, attribution, freshness, conflict, professional-review, internal-only, and blocked-use prompts without public exposure or conclusions.

2. Did it sufficiently prove the Evidence Depth foundation?

Yes, for internal operating standards and future bounded integrations. It did not authorize public evidence labels, providers, acquisition, persistence, public records, customer-specific evidence packets, or substantive conclusions.

3. What customer and business gaps remain?

The largest gap is consistent human use of REIE: advisors need a governed operating standard for applying certified journey surfaces, evidence limitations, question categories, and professional boundaries without CRM, tracking, or customer profiling.

4. Should REIE now prioritize internal operating leverage or another public product?

Internal operating leverage. The current public guidance/readiness/handoff layer is certified, and no repository evidence shows a stronger immediate public-product friction.

5. Is Advisory Operating Readiness the strongest next initiative?

Yes. It has the best combination of business value, customer-experience consistency, architectural readiness, reuse of certified foundations, velocity, certification simplicity, and protected-boundary safety.

6. Can Advisory Operating Readiness proceed safely without CRM, tracking, or customer data?

Yes, if scoped to internal standards, checklists, contracts, fixtures, and deterministic validation only. It must not implement CRM, customer records, lead scoring, lead routing, tracking, telemetry, saved preparation, automated outreach, transaction management, or advisor performance scoring.

7. Is Neighborhood / Submarket Architecture ready now?

It is ready for future architecture-only work, but not as the primary next initiative and not for public route activation.

8. Is Property / Seller Evidence Readiness ready now?

It is feasible later as an internal, fixture-backed evidence-category contract, but it is not ready to outrank Advisory Operating Readiness because property identity, ownership/privacy, provider/public-record, valuation, condition, and professional-boundary risks remain material.

9. Is a second Controlled Evidence integration necessary?

No. The first integration sufficiently proved the foundation. A second integration should occur only when tied to a specific domain need.

10. Does the evidence architecture change the need for Decision Guide unification?

No immediate change. Mixed-maturity comparison remains certified, and editorial Decision Guides should not be migrated or relabeled without separate evidence.

11. Should Niwot and LDI Wave 4 remain paused?

Yes. Niwot and similar markets should wait for Neighborhood / Submarket or geographic-object governance.

12. Is targeted Product Experience work justified?

Not currently. It should be evidence-triggered by production friction, not started as another broad cohesion wave.

13. What should Codex implement next?

Advisory Operating Readiness, after explicit authorization.

14. What should Codex not implement yet?

Codex should not implement public evidence labels, public neighborhood/submarket routes, Property / Seller Evidence public products, Decision Guide migration, Niwot, provider activation, public records, GIS, acquisition, persistence, APIs, schema, CRM, tracking, telemetry, customer data, AI, personalization, valuation, pricing, affordability, qualification, scoring, rankings, forecasts, or production writes.

15. What evidence would cause the priority to change?

Measured product friction, source-rights/provider clearance, verified neighborhood/submarket demand, safe property-evidence prerequisites, Decision Guide confusion, resolved Niwot/Gunbarrel object governance, or evidence that advisory operations are already consistent without a standard.
