## 5. Remaining Seller Gaps

Seller gaps remain, but they are not the best immediate next public implementation:

- deeper property or seller evidence would require source-rights, public-record, provider, or qualified-source governance before any property-specific conclusions.
- valuation, pricing, price bands, seller net sheets, and comparable-sale calculations remain explicitly unauthorized.
- seller readiness remains non-persistent and does not collect documents or property records.
- seller follow-up automation, CRM workflows, alerts, and email remain inactive.

The next seller-side value is evidence depth or qualified advisor workflow, but those paths carry higher valuation, source-rights, privacy, and operational risk than a buyer financing-readiness education layer.

## 6. Remaining Buyer Gaps

The largest remaining buyer gap is financing readiness.

The current system already provides Buyer Guidance, Financing Confidence education, Search, property Financial Context, Market Context, Grand Plan, and Advisory continuity. Buyers still need a more organized workspace to understand what to gather, what to ask, and what assumptions require professional review before they treat a price range or property as financially actionable.

Unresolved buyer questions:

- What information should I gather before a lender or advisor conversation?
- Which financing assumptions should I verify before touring or offering?
- How should I think about cash-to-close, reserves, taxes, insurance, HOA dues, PMI, maintenance, and rate assumptions without receiving a payment quote?
- Which questions belong with a lender, real-estate advisor, insurer, tax professional, or attorney?
- How should financing readiness connect back to Search, market context, Grand Plan, and Advisory without becoming a lending funnel?

## 7. Initiatives Evaluated

### Buyer Financing Readiness Advancement

Finding: highest-priority next implementation candidate.

Buyer Financing Readiness is the strongest complement to Seller Readiness. It can reuse Buyer Guidance, Financing Confidence education, Search, Market Context, Grand Plan, and Advisory while staying education-first and protected-boundary safe. The correct initial scope is readiness and question organization, not calculators, rates, loan products, lender matching, qualification, or affordability conclusions.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: useful later, not urgent.

Mixed-maturity comparison remains certified and safe. Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette can continue as editorially certified without immediate migration to `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`. Unification may reduce future validation complexity, but it is not needed before Buyer Financing Readiness and could flatten editorial depth if treated as a mechanical migration.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence

Finding: strategically important but not ready now.

Neighborhood and submarket intelligence has high customer and SEO value, but object-type governance, route eligibility, classification, source evidence, fair-housing and steering controls, Gunbarrel-like ambiguity, and certification scale remain material blockers. Existing Neighborhood Product 3.0 evidence supports cautious neighborhood presentation, not broad public expansion.

### Evidence Depth And Data Integration

Finding: enterprise-critical, but not required before a readiness-only buyer implementation.

Evidence depth would improve reliability and future property, seller, buyer, neighborhood, and geographic intelligence. Source-rights and Geographic Intelligence records still gate provider activation, public-record ingestion, public GIS, and production writes. A bounded read-only evidence phase should precede deeper property-specific, valuation, provider-backed, or neighborhood intelligence, but it does not need to precede education-first Buyer Financing Readiness.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

The public `FOUNDATION` backlog is complete. Niwot still requires authority, object-type, route, and search-support reconciliation. Additional city expansion has lower immediate customer value than closing the buyer financing-readiness gap.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: important later, not next.

A property or seller evidence-readiness layer could strengthen Seller Readiness and Property Intelligence, but it risks drifting into property conclusions, valuation, source-rights activation, public-record acquisition, and provider dependency. It should follow a more explicit evidence governance phase.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: no broad follow-up justified now.

Product Experience, Cross-City Comparison, and Seller Readiness have recently improved discovery, continuity, and advisory pathways. A targeted follow-up may be justified later if production evidence shows a specific friction point, but no current repository record demonstrates that another broad cohesion wave outranks Buyer Financing Readiness.

### Other Repository-Supported Priorities

The repository contains credible longer-term programs across Geographic Intelligence, source-rights readiness, Decision Guide unification, neighborhood products, and enterprise adapters. None currently outranks a protected-boundary-safe Buyer Financing Readiness Advancement for immediate customer value, business value, architecture reuse, implementation velocity, and certifiability.
