## Documentation-Only Terminology Standard

The certified disposition remains:

`DOCUMENTATION_ONLY_TERMINOLOGY_STANDARD`

Definitions:

- `Available now`: evidence or context already visible on the route and safe to rely on for orientation.
- `Needs verification`: material point that requires source, property, lender, market, or professional review before acting.
- `Not confirmed here`: a route-local limitation stating that the current page does not verify the fact.
- `Assumption`: an explicit working premise that helps the customer understand what is being treated as provisional.
- `Unknown from current evidence`: a material gap that the current route cannot resolve.
- `Qualitative confidence`: preparation-focused confidence based on evidence completeness, freshness, visibility of assumptions, and limitation severity; never a score, grade, approval, valuation, suitability label, or recommendation.
- `Freshness`: the route-specific recency or currency posture of the evidence, shown without false precision where live source freshness is not established.
- `Verification`: the customer-facing indication of what should be checked through source records, property review, lender review, market review, or professional review.
- `Next-decision threshold`: a descriptive threshold for deciding what to inspect, compare, refine, verify, prepare, or discuss next; it must not prescribe a purchase, sale, listing, pricing, financing, investment, or suitability decision.
- `Professional review`: the boundary between public decision preparation and advice or representation that requires an appropriate professional relationship or source-specific review.

The standard allows route-specific language where customer decisions differ. It does not require exact copy uniformity.

## Findings

Intentional route-specific differences are expected and accepted. Search, Property, Buyer, Seller, Market, Neighborhood, Advisory, and Contact use different evidence language because they answer different customer decisions.

Material inconsistencies do not require runtime implementation at this stage. The primary gap is terminology governance and future certification consistency, not customer-facing breakage.

Customer impact is best addressed by documentation-level standards and deterministic checks. Cosmetic copy uniformity is not the objective.

Direct-entry behavior remains mandatory for every route. Context may orient a customer but must not be required for route comprehension or functionality.
