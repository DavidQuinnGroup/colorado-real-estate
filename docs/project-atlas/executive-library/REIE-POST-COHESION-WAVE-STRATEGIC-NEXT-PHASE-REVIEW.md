## 4. Post-Cohesion Interpretation

The Product Experience Next Cohesion Wave resolved the largest gap identified by the prior strategic review: the platform no longer depends only on isolated certified surfaces. The customer journey is now clearer across homepage, buyer, seller, home-worth, Grand Plan, advisory, city, market, search, property, neighborhood, financing, trust, and disclosure surfaces.

Resolved or materially improved friction:

- homepage journey entrances are clearer
- Buy, Sell, Home Worth, Grand Plan, and Contact / Advisory have shared continuation panels
- CTA labels and destinations are governed more consistently
- city-to-search continuity and browser Back behavior remain certified
- Search-to-property continuity remains functional
- certified Local Decision Intelligence continuity identities remain intact
- product-experience integration is no longer the dominant immediate bottleneck

Problems that remain:

- customers can review individual cities but cannot yet compare city tradeoffs in one neutral decision workspace
- nine governed city/Decision Guide experiences exist with no cross-city synthesis layer
- Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette remain editorially certified while six other public cities use `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`
- neighborhood/submarket intelligence remains high-value but not route/object-type ready
- evidence depth and source-rights work remain strategically important but provider and legal gates are not ready for public activation
- seller valuation and buyer financing can create conversion value, but they carry higher claim, compliance, calculator, lender, and personal-finance risk than a neutral comparison layer

The post-cohesion conclusion is that REIE now needs a deeper decision surface, not another navigation-focused wave.

## 5. Initiatives Evaluated

### Cross-City Decision Comparison

Potential scope: a governed customer experience that helps compare public city markets without rankings, winner declarations, suitability conclusions, demographic steering, school/safety scoring, forecasting, valuation, or investment guidance.

Finding: highest-value next implementation candidate.

Rationale:

- directly uses the six `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` cities and three editorial Decision Guides already certified
- reuses Decision Snapshots, Local Character, Market Drivers, maturity, evidence limitations, and journey contracts
- benefits from the newly certified journey cohesion layer because comparison can now connect naturally to Search, Market, Buy, Sell, Financing, Grand Plan, and Advisory
- has strong customer value because many real estate decisions are comparative before they are property-specific
- can be implemented as neutral decision support without live data, provider activation, valuation, personalization, ranking, or new city eligibility

Required constraints:

- no rankings
- no "best city" or winner language
- no suitability claims
- no demographic targeting or protected-class proxies
- no school or safety scoring
- no appreciation, investment, or forecast claims
- no new city or neighborhood routes unless separately authorized
- maturity differences must remain visible
- editorial Decision Guides can participate as editorially certified entries without being migrated first

Decision Guide unification is not a prerequisite. Cross-city comparison can disclose mixed maturity and treat editorial cities as higher-depth context rather than forcing all cities into one maturity class.

### Boulder, Louisville, And Lafayette Decision Guide Unification

Potential scope: reconcile Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette editorial Decision Guides with the `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` city architecture.

Finding: useful later, not the next implementation priority.

Rationale:

- could reduce differences across the nine public governed city experiences
- could make future comparison and validation simpler
- risks reducing the distinct editorial depth of Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette if treated as a mechanical migration
- is not required for cross-city comparison if comparison explicitly displays maturity and evidence boundaries

Recommended posture:

- companion planning consideration for cross-city comparison
- not a prerequisite
- not a standalone immediate implementation unless comparison planning discovers unavoidable contract gaps

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence

Potential scope: governed layer below city intelligence, including neighborhood, subdivision, market-area, and ambiguous communities such as Gunbarrel.

Finding: strategically important but not immediate.

Rationale:

- high customer and SEO value
- high risk around object type, neighborhood versus market-area classification, route governance, fair-housing controls, and steering avoidance
- existing Geographic Intelligence roadmap keeps customer activation unauthorized
- Gunbarrel and similar entities need classification before public intelligence can be expanded

Recommended posture:

- queue for separate architecture planning
- do not combine with cross-city comparison
- do not create neighborhood routes during the next wave

### Evidence Depth And Data Integration

Potential scope: source rights, provenance, freshness, public-record depth, market-data depth, and provider readiness.

Finding: high enterprise leverage but not the next public implementation.

Rationale:

- source-rights records approve existing repository-local MLS-derived market intelligence but keep government, assessor, permit, recorder, planning, imagery, and provider sources gated by legal/provider review
- evidence depth has large long-term value but carries the highest avoidable protected-boundary and provider risk
- public comparison can proceed with existing governed evidence if it is limitation-forward and does not imply live freshness or provider-derived conclusions

Recommended posture:

- proceed later as a source-rights/provider-readiness program
- do not block a bounded comparison experience on unavailable evidence depth
- do not activate external acquisition, public GIS, provider data, or production writes

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Potential scope: Niwot authority and search-support reconciliation.

Finding: remain paused on a slower governed cadence.

Rationale:

- public `FOUNDATION` backlog is complete
- Niwot remains blocked by search-support and unincorporated-community governance considerations
- incremental customer value is narrower than a comparison layer across all certified cities
- route/registry eligibility should not be weakened to produce another public city page

Recommended posture:

- keep queued for future planning
- do not activate Niwot in the next implementation wave

### Seller Valuation / Home-Worth Advancement

Potential scope: improve seller-readiness or home-worth value without creating unsupported valuation conclusions.

Finding: valuable but higher claim risk than cross-city comparison.

Rationale:

- Home Worth now has certified cohesion and valuation limitations
- conversion value is real, but the next step can easily drift into automated valuation, pricing claims, regulatory risk, or provider dependencies
- a protected-boundary-safe seller-readiness enhancement could be valuable later, but it should not be combined with cross-city comparison

Recommended posture:

- queue for seller-readiness planning after comparison, or earlier if lead/conversion evidence shows seller friction dominates
- do not implement valuation logic or price conclusions

### Buyer Financing-Readiness Advancement

Potential scope: deepen Financing Confidence without calculators, rates, qualification, or lender integration.

Finding: valuable but dependency and compliance sensitive.

Rationale:

- the existing Financing Confidence experience is education-first and protected-boundary safe
- a deeper readiness workflow could improve confidence and conversion
- calculator, rate, lender, advertising, personal financial intake, and qualification risks remain material

Recommended posture:

- queue for separate protected-boundary-safe planning
- do not combine with cross-city comparison

## 7. Primary Priority Decision

Primary priority finding:

`PRIORITIZE_CROSS_CITY_DECISION_COMPARISON`

Recommended next implementation initiative:

`CROSS_CITY_DECISION_COMPARISON_IMPLEMENTATION_AUTHORIZATION`

Recommended implementation objective:

Create a governed Cross-City Decision Comparison experience that helps customers compare certified Colorado city contexts through neutral decision dimensions, explicit maturity/evidence boundaries, and continuation paths to Search, Market/city pages, Buyer Guidance, Seller Guidance, Financing Guidance, Grand Plan, and Advisory.

Recommended initial scope:

- use existing certified public city and Decision Guide records only
- include the six `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` cities: Broomfield, Superior, Longmont, Denver, Erie, Westminster
- include Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette only with their existing `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` maturity disclosed
- build neutral comparison dimensions from existing Decision Snapshot, Local Character, Market Drivers, evidence limitations, and journey contracts
- provide city-specific search and market links
- provide buyer, seller, financing, Grand Plan, and advisory continuity
- include clear limitation language that comparison is not a ranking, recommendation, suitability determination, forecast, valuation, investment analysis, school/safety assessment, demographic filter, or neighborhood certification
- add deterministic validation for labels, destination semantics, maturity disclosure, prohibited claims, fair-housing boundaries, protected-boundary attributes, and regression across certified routes

Recommended exclusions:

- no city rankings
- no winner or "best fit" declarations
- no suitability, demographic, school, safety, investment, appreciation, valuation, or urgency claims
- no personalization, AI, telemetry, tracking, cookies, or customer profiling
- no provider activation, public GIS, external acquisition, schema, Prisma, migration, API, search-ranking, map-boundary, alert, queue, worker, email, or customer-data changes
- no Niwot, Gunbarrel, Thornton, Brighton, Firestone, Frederick, or new Local Decision Intelligence expansion
- no neighborhood/submarket intelligence
- no Decision Guide unification unless separately authorized
- no seller valuation or financing-readiness advancement in the same implementation wave

## 14. Required Questions Answered

1. What did the Product Experience cohesion wave accomplish?

It made the certified public surfaces work more like one guided decision journey by clarifying homepage entrances, adding shared continuation panels to Buy, Sell, Home Worth, Grand Plan, and Contact / Advisory, preserving city-to-search and search-to-property continuity, and certifying responsive and protected-boundary behavior.

2. Which meaningful customer problems remain?

Customers can review strong individual city, market, property, neighborhood, buyer, seller, financing, and advisory surfaces, but they still lack a neutral way to compare city tradeoffs across the certified coverage area.

3. What is now the largest gap between REIE capability and customer value?

The largest gap is cross-city synthesis: REIE has enough certified city intelligence to help customers compare markets, but it does not yet provide a governed comparison experience.

4. Which initiative best uses the newly improved journey architecture?

Cross-City Decision Comparison best uses the newly improved journey architecture because it can sit naturally between homepage, market/city intelligence, search, buyer/seller guidance, financing confidence, Grand Plan, and advisory.

5. Does cross-city comparison remain the strongest next candidate?

Yes. It moves from second priority to first after the cohesion wave because the integration prerequisite is now certified.

6. Is Decision Guide unification a prerequisite, a companion task, or unnecessary?

It is a companion consideration, not a prerequisite. Mixed maturity can be disclosed. Editorial Decision Guides should not be flattened unless a later migration standard is explicitly approved.

7. Should evidence depth precede new intelligence experiences?

Not for bounded cross-city comparison. Evidence depth should precede provider-derived, live, public-record, GIS, valuation, or high-specificity experiences, but existing certified city intelligence is sufficient for neutral comparison with explicit limitations.

8. Should Local Decision Intelligence expansion remain paused?

Yes. Expansion should remain on a slower governed cadence until Niwot and other candidates resolve authority, search-support, route, and classification questions.

9. What should Codex implement next?

After explicit authorization, Codex should implement a governed Cross-City Decision Comparison experience with neutral dimensions, maturity/evidence transparency, exact journey continuity, and deterministic safety checks.

10. What should Codex not implement yet?

Codex should not implement Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4, Niwot, Gunbarrel, neighborhood/submarket intelligence, Decision Guide unification, evidence/provider integration, seller valuation logic, financing calculators, lender workflows, AI, personalization, telemetry, rankings, forecasts, valuation, APIs, schema changes, search-ranking changes, map-boundary changes, alerts, queues, workers, email, or production mutation.

11. What evidence would cause the priority to change?

The priority should change if customer/search evidence shows materially greater demand for a blocked city, seller-route friction materially exceeds comparison friction, compliance approvals unlock a protected-boundary-safe seller or financing capability, source-rights/provider approvals make evidence depth unusually ready, or executive strategy directs focus toward a specific revenue path with stronger near-term value.

## 15. Risks And Dependencies

| Risk | Level | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cross-city comparison implies ranking or winner selection | High | Use neutral dimensions, no totals, no sorting by superiority, and deterministic prohibited-claim checks. |
| Comparison creates protected-class or demographic steering | High | Restrict dimensions to property, market, access, housing form, evidence limitation, and due-diligence language; prohibit school/safety/demographic/suitability copy. |
| Mixed maturity confuses customers | Moderate | Display `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION` and `EDITORIALLY_CERTIFIED` maturity separately with plain limitation language. |
| Editorial Decision Guides are flattened unintentionally | Moderate | Treat Boulder, Louisville, and Lafayette as editorial entries; do not migrate them inside the comparison implementation. |
| New comparison route becomes too broad | Moderate | Limit initial scope to existing certified public cities and journey continuity. |
| Evidence depth is overstated | Moderate | Use already certified city/guide content and include limitation-forward copy. |
| Scope drifts into cross-city rankings, AI, personalization, or provider data | High | Require explicit protected-boundary attributes and deterministic validation. |
| Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4 resumes by momentum | Moderate | Keep Niwot and later cities queued only; do not change city registry or eligibility. |
| Seller or financing work is combined into comparison | Moderate | Keep seller and financing as destination continuity only, not new capability scope. |
