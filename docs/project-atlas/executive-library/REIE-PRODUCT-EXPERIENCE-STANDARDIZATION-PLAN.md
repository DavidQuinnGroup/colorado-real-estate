## 4. Product Experience Purpose

The REIE Product Experience Standard exists to keep customer-facing work:

- clear;
- calm;
- decision-led;
- mobile-first;
- trust-aware;
- limitation-forward;
- easy to continue from;
- protected from unauthorized data, provider, CRM, personalization, and scoring behavior.

The standard should help future initiatives answer:

- What is the one primary customer decision on this surface?
- What must the first screen clarify?
- What action should dominate?
- What should remain secondary?
- What preparation or verification should precede action?
- What claims, calculations, scores, or recommendations are prohibited?
- What should stay surface-specific rather than becoming a shared pattern?

## 5. Reusable Patterns Inventoried

Reusable patterns certified across the recent Product Experience portfolio:

- one clear surface purpose;
- first-screen hierarchy with one dominant action;
- mobile-first single-column narrative where decision clarity matters;
- restrained secondary actions;
- preparation before higher-commitment action;
- concise editorial copy;
- meaningful whitespace;
- readable line lengths;
- tonal fields used sparingly;
- ordinary explanatory border-heavy boxes materially reduced;
- no dashboard, scorecard, intake-portal, financial-institution, or CRM appearance;
- prompt-only questions where uncertainty must be explored;
- deterministic questions that do not become conclusions;
- clear professional-boundary language;
- trust and privacy language placed near relevant actions;
- continuation links preserved without dense route clusters;
- no hidden state transfer;
- no unauthorized persistence;
- no provider dependency;
- no unsupported public intelligence claims;
- deterministic validation of product boundaries;
- responsive, accessibility, interaction, regression, and protected-boundary certification.

## 6. Enterprise Standards Identified

The following patterns become enterprise Product Experience standards.

### Purpose Standard

Every customer-facing REIE surface must state one primary purpose.

Acceptable secondary purposes are limited and subordinate. A page must not attempt to summarize the entire platform when its job is to orient, prepare, calculate, verify, search, compare, or transition.

### First-Screen Standard

The first meaningful viewport must establish:

- where the customer is;
- what decision or preparation step this surface supports;
- one dominant action;
- no more than one subordinate action where practical;
- no dense route-choice cluster;
- no legal wall above the primary experience;
- no dashboard, scorecard, intake, CRM, or approval visual language.

### Hierarchy Standard

Default section order should follow:

1. orientation;
2. customer purpose;
3. primary action or primary preparation;
4. supporting explanation;
5. trust, privacy, or professional boundaries where relevant;
6. continuation paths;
7. lower-priority supporting content.

Specific surfaces may adapt this order only when repository evidence shows the customer decision requires a different sequence.

### Mobile-First Standard

Mobile is the baseline, not a compressed desktop layout.

At approximately `390x844`, future experiences should show:

- clear first-screen purpose;
- readable hierarchy;
- stable touch targets;
- one dominant action per section where practical;
- no horizontal overflow;
- no clipped or overlapping content;
- no dense tables unless the surface is explicitly tabular;
- optional content progressively disclosed when it would crowd the flow.

### CTA Standard

CTA hierarchy should use:

- one primary action;
- restrained secondary action;
- low-priority continuation links;
- no dense CTA clusters;
- no provider, lender, scheduling, upload, application, qualification, or contact-infrastructure CTA unless separately authorized.

### Editorial Rhythm Standard

Future surfaces should use:

- short section openings;
- compact supporting copy;
- one idea per visible block;
- meaningful whitespace between decisions;
- lower-density continuation content;
- no repeated explanations that compete with the primary action.

### Boundary Standard

When professional, privacy, evidence, financing, valuation, or advisory limits matter, boundaries must appear close to the relevant interaction or claim.

Boundaries should be readable and progressive, not dense legal walls.

### Prompt-Only Standard

Questions used for decision support must remain prompts.

They must not become:

- conclusions;
- recommendations;
- rankings;
- readiness labels;
- scores;
- grades;
- approval indicators;
- suitability determinations;
- proceed or delay advice.

### No Hidden Transfer Standard

Future surfaces must not infer or transfer customer context, planner inputs, Search behavior, referral URLs, profile data, financial assumptions, or journey history unless explicitly authorized by a later governance gate.

### Validation Standard

Every future Product Experience implementation should define deterministic checks for:

- placement and route boundaries;
- primary CTA;
- prohibited claims;
- prohibited scoring or labels;
- no unauthorized persistence;
- no hidden transfer;
- required disclosures;
- journey continuity;
- responsive and accessibility expectations.

## 8. Design Language

The REIE enterprise design language should be:

- calm;
- editorial;
- premium without ornament;
- decision-led;
- evidence-aware;
- whitespace-forward;
- mobile-first;
- limitation-forward;
- accessible;
- restrained.

Future experiences should feel like guided intelligence, not an operations dashboard.

Preferred composition:

- clear headings;
- short explanatory copy;
- wide editorial spacing;
- modest tonal separation;
- concise prompts;
- purposeful CTAs;
- compact continuations;
- trust and boundary text close to the relevant action.

Avoid:

- dense card walls;
- excessive borders;
- decorative gradients;
- heavy shadows;
- scorecards;
- approval meters;
- CRM or intake portals;
- bank or lender visual language;
- crowded route menus;
- legal-wall presentation;
- internal evidence metadata;
- provider names or IDs unless separately authorized.

## 9. Page Hierarchy Standards

Future REIE pages should use a decision hierarchy before a content inventory hierarchy.

Recommended default:

1. orientation;
2. primary customer question;
3. dominant next step;
4. preparation or assumptions if needed;
5. explanation of how REIE helps;
6. prompts or items to verify;
7. trust, privacy, evidence, or professional boundaries;
8. advisory or continuation transition;
9. lower-priority research continuations;
10. existing footer.

Pages may deviate only when repository evidence shows a different customer decision order.

## 10. First-Screen Standards

The first screen should not include:

- competing route clusters;
- long disclaimers;
- dense forms;
- deep internal architecture terminology;
- unrelated platform summaries;
- dashboards;
- large contact-method walls;
- scorecards;
- unactionable evidence metadata.

The first screen should include:

- concise heading;
- one customer-centered supporting idea;
- primary action;
- optional secondary action if justified;
- restrained trust cue where relevant.

## 11. CTA Standards

CTA levels:

- primary: the single action most aligned with the surface purpose;
- secondary: a direct alternative or continuation that does not compete visually;
- tertiary: compact research or route continuation links.

Rules:

- no more than one dominant CTA per section where practical;
- no more than two visible CTAs per mobile viewport where practical;
- no duplicate CTAs that send the customer to the same destination without a distinct role;
- no lender, provider, scheduling, upload, application, qualification, CRM, or telemetry CTA without explicit authorization;
- no CTA should imply approval, qualification, affordability, valuation certainty, suitability, or professional advice.

## 12. Content Standards

Customer-facing copy should be:

- concise;
- concrete;
- customer-centered;
- preparation-oriented;
- limitation-aware;
- free of internal enterprise terminology;
- free of superiority claims.

Avoid:

- best or ideal claims;
- neighborhood desirability;
- demographic targeting;
- protected-class proxies;
- safety, crime, or school rankings;
- affordability conclusions;
- valuation conclusions;
- investment advice;
- rate guarantees;
- lending approval implications;
- provider recommendations;
- internal evidence IDs;
- source IDs;
- rights enums;
- maturity codes;
- confidence percentages;
- scores;
- grades;
- eligibility labels.

## 13. Trust And Disclosure Standards

Trust presentation should be integrated into the decision flow.

Standards:

- place disclosures near relevant outputs or actions;
- use concise language before expanding into detail;
- distinguish educational estimate, prompt, or preparation from advice;
- state user-entered assumptions when calculations appear;
- state professional verification when required;
- avoid dense legal-wall presentation;
- do not overclaim backend behavior that repository evidence cannot prove.

Privacy messaging should distinguish:

- certified current behavior;
- desired future messaging;
- prohibited future behavior.

Do not claim:

- absence of all analytics across the platform unless verified and authorized;
- absence of all CRM in the business unless verified and authorized;
- legal confidentiality guarantees unless verified and authorized;
- backend behavior that is not supported by repository evidence.

## 14. Professional-Boundary Standards

REIE experiences must not independently determine:

- legal status;
- title;
- ownership;
- permits;
- zoning;
- HOA obligations;
- insurance eligibility or coverage;
- structural or environmental condition;
- financing approval;
- tax treatment;
- legal advice;
- valuation certainty;
- affordability;
- buying power;
- suitability;
- investment performance.

When relevant, surfaces should direct customers to qualified professionals without becoming provider recommendation engines.

## 15. Evidence And Source-Rights Standards

Evidence-related experiences must preserve:

- controlled evidence boundaries;
- source-rights readiness;
- provider and source attribution rules where authorized;
- no internal IDs in public copy;
- no unsupported conclusions;
- no maturity or confidence codes in customer-facing text unless specifically authorized;
- no evidence expansion without provenance and source-rights readiness.

## 16. Mobile-First Standards

At mobile widths, future experiences should:

- use a single-column narrative unless the content is inherently tabular;
- put the customer decision first;
- avoid compressed desktop grids;
- keep optional content collapsed or lower priority;
- avoid long card walls;
- avoid sticky controls unless proven necessary;
- keep disclosures adjacent to relevant outputs;
- keep CTAs large enough for touch;
- preserve visible focus states;
- avoid horizontal overflow;
- avoid clipped content.

At tablet and desktop widths, future experiences should:

- preserve readable line lengths;
- use restrained widths;
- use two-column layouts only for complementary content;
- avoid excessive empty regions;
- avoid dashboard density;
- preserve editorial rhythm.

## 17. Accessibility Standards

Every Product Experience surface should preserve or improve:

- semantic heading order;
- keyboard operation;
- logical tab order;
- visible focus states;
- explicit labels for controls;
- accessible names for CTAs;
- screen-reader-readable disclosures;
- no color-only meaning;
- stable touch targets;
- reduced-motion compatibility where relevant;
- no address-bar/rendered-state desynchronization;
- no dead retained links.

Accessibility should be validated with both static checks and interaction review when runtime work is separately authorized.

## 18. Continuation Standards

Continuation links should help the customer move forward without turning the page into a route directory.

Allowed continuation categories:

- Search;
- Buy;
- Sell;
- Market;
- Grand Plan;
- Advisory;
- surface-specific existing routes where justified.

Rules:

- keep continuation links compact;
- group by customer intent, not internal architecture;
- avoid duplicate route choices;
- avoid unverified new destinations;
- preserve existing governed journeys;
- do not create routes without explicit authorization.

## 19. Progressive Disclosure Standards

Use progressive disclosure when:

- optional assumptions would crowd required inputs;
- disclosures would become a legal wall;
- journey topics are useful but secondary;
- questions-to-verify are long enough to disrupt the main flow;
- mobile density would otherwise increase.

Do not use progressive disclosure to hide:

- required warnings;
- primary purpose;
- required input labels;
- validation errors;
- professional boundaries directly tied to an output or action.

## 20. Component Density, Borders, And Tonal Fields

Density standard:

- ordinary explanatory text should float through typography, spacing, and alignment;
- repeated dense cards should be avoided unless the content is genuinely a repeated item set;
- card walls should not be used as the default organization model;
- compact grouped content should remain readable on mobile.

Border standard:

- ordinary explanatory boxes should not rely on visible borders as the main grouping method;
- functional controls may retain boundaries;
- focus states must remain visible;
- intentionally interactive elements may retain borders where useful.

Tonal-field standard:

- restrained tonal fields may separate major sections;
- tonal fields should not become decorative gradients or heavy visual effects;
- shadows should be minimal and functional when used.
