Largest remaining gap: customers can now compare, prepare, and organize questions, but the final transition into human advisory support is still generic relative to the intelligence they just consumed.

Remaining problems:

- Contact / Advisory is available, but it does not yet fully convert the certified journey surfaces into a coherent advisory preparation handoff.
- Users may not know which questions to bring forward from Comparison, Seller Readiness, Buyer Financing Readiness, Grand Plan, Search, Market Context, property, or neighborhood review.

## 8. Initiatives Evaluated

### Advisory Handoff Readiness

Finding: highest-value next implementation candidate.

Advisory Handoff now has the strongest combination of immediate customer value, conversion value, reuse, implementation velocity, and protected-boundary safety. Evidence Depth reduces the prior concern that advisory handoff would be merely a cosmetic contact improvement. A bounded implementation can help users prepare better human conversations from existing certified surfaces without forms, CRM automation, tracking, customer-data persistence, or high-pressure funnel behavior.

Recommended scope should be presentational, non-persistent, and non-automated.

### Controlled Evidence Depth Integration

Finding: second-priority initiative.

A controlled integration is architecturally ready, but it should not be the primary next implementation because the foundation was just certified and the next highest value is applying its language to customer decision handoff without exposing internal metadata. The safest first integration target is Advisory preparation: use evidence-aware limitation and question-preparation framing, not raw public evidence labels, scores, or conclusions.

### Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture

Finding: strategically important, not primary now.

Evidence Depth helps future neighborhood work, but public neighborhood/submarket expansion still needs object-type rules, Gunbarrel-like ambiguity handling, registry and route governance, source-rights posture, fair-housing/steering safeguards, and certification scalability.

### Property / Seller Evidence Readiness

Finding: important but premature as a standalone public initiative.

Evidence Depth makes a non-public fixture-backed evidence-category phase more feasible, but property/seller evidence still risks drifting into valuation, condition, title, insurance, inspection, legal, tax, ownership, or disclosure conclusions. It should not precede advisory handoff unless scoped purely as internal evidence-category work.

### Decision Guide Unification

Finding: not necessary now.

Mixed-maturity comparison remains certified and safe. Evidence Depth does not create a new urgent need to relabel Boulder, Louisville, or Lafayette or force them into `ENHANCED_FOUNDATION`. Unification should remain queued until customer or operational evidence shows mixed maturity is creating material friction.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

Niwot still presents authority, object-type, route, registry, relationship, and search-support questions. Evidence Depth may help later, but Niwot likely belongs in neighborhood/submarket or geographic-object governance rather than city-level expansion.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: no broad wave justified.

Recent certified product work already improved homepage, journey cohesion, comparison, seller readiness, buyer readiness, and evidence governance. A targeted follow-up is justified only if production evidence identifies specific discovery, CTA, mobile, or handoff friction. Advisory Handoff is the specific product-facing gap now supported by repository evidence.

### Other Repository-Supported Priorities

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks Advisory Handoff Readiness after Evidence Depth closure.

## 12. Advisory Handoff Finding

Advisory Handoff Readiness is now the strongest next candidate.

Rationale:

- Comparison, Seller Readiness, Buyer Financing Readiness, Grand Plan, Market Context, and Contact already point toward advisory guidance.
- Evidence Depth now supplies the internal vocabulary needed to frame what should be verified without exposing internal metadata publicly.
- The implementation can be presentational, static/server-rendered, non-persistent, and low operational risk.
- Business value is high because clearer handoff improves qualified consultation readiness without requiring CRM automation.
- Certification should be simpler than provider, evidence, property, neighborhood, or public-record work.

## 13. Controlled Evidence Integration Finding

Controlled Evidence Depth integration is ready as a bounded second-priority initiative, but should not be the primary next implementation.

Safe integration principles:

- use one domain only.
- prefer Advisory preparation as the first target.
- keep the integration non-conclusive and non-scoring.
- do not expose raw internal evidence metadata as customer-facing certainty.
- do not activate providers, acquisition, persistence, APIs, schema, production writes, or public records.

## 14. Neighborhood / Submarket Finding

Neighborhood / Submarket Intelligence Architecture is not the next implementation priority.

It remains queued because it needs object-type governance, source-rights mapping, evidence posture, route/registry architecture, Gunbarrel-like ambiguity resolution, fair-housing and steering safeguards, and a scalable certification strategy before public expansion.

## 15. Property / Seller Evidence Finding

Property / Seller Evidence Readiness should not begin as a public product now.

It may become a controlled internal evidence-category phase after Advisory Handoff or as part of a future controlled Evidence Depth integration. It must remain clear of ownership lookup, address lookup, provider calls, public-record retrieval, uploads, valuation, condition, title, insurance, inspection, legal, tax, lending, appraisal, or disclosure conclusions unless separately authorized and certified.
