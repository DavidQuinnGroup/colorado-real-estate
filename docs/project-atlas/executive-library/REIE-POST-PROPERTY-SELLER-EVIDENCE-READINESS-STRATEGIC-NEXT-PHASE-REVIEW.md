## 5. Post-Property / Seller Evidence Assessment

Property / Seller Evidence should remain internal for now. The foundation is valuable precisely because it does not cross into customer-specific evidence packets, lookup, records retrieval, uploads, valuation, condition conclusions, or public display.

A second Property / Seller evidence phase is not justified as the next primary initiative unless it is tied to a specific future operating surface, advisor workflow authorization, or public-product prerequisite. Repeating category, rights, freshness, and conflict modeling without a concrete activation path would create diminishing returns.

The largest remaining customer and business gap has shifted back to neighborhood/submarket depth. REIE now has city-level public intelligence, Decision Guide evidence transparency, property/seller evidence readiness, and a first governed internal neighborhood wave. Customers still lack richer governed sub-city context between city-level guidance and property-level experiences, while the business still has SEO and local-authority opportunity in existing neighborhood contexts.

## 6. Initiative Findings

### Second Governed Neighborhood / Submarket Wave

Finding: strongest next implementation priority.

A second internal wave can create new value if it is not a repeat of Wave 1. The next wave should expand repository-supported candidate inventory, reconcile additional identities and aliases, deepen parent, child, overlap, market, municipal, and county relationships, add existing-route preservation cases, identify bounded future certification candidates, improve evidence, rights, authority, boundary, Search, and map readiness, and preserve Gunbarrel and Niwot blockers where unresolved.

The second wave should remain internal. It should create no public routes, route eligibility, registry eligibility, public metadata, Search changes, map changes, sitemap changes, canonical changes, public content changes, rankings, scores, desirability claims, school or safety conclusions, valuation, forecasts, or neighborhood conclusions.

Safest object types for the next geographic phase:

- existing repository-supported `NEIGHBORHOOD` contexts
- repository-supported `SUBDIVISION` records held internal-only unless separately certified
- bounded `MARKET_AREA` context for internal relationship posture
- governance-only `COMMUNITY` or `UNINCORPORATED_COMMUNITY` references where needed for relationship context, without public city or Decision Guide activation

### Existing Neighborhood Route Enhancement

Finding: high-value secondary priority, not ready as the next implementation without another internal wave.

An existing public route such as `/market/boulder/downtown-boulder`, Mapleton Hill, North Boulder, or another repository-supported existing neighborhood route may become a strong public enhancement candidate. The strongest bounded future scope would be evidence and limitation transparency, object identity and geographic-scope clarification, and stronger city, Search, property, Grand Plan, and Advisory continuity while preserving route, canonical, sitemap, Search, and map behavior.

The current evidence supports readiness assessment, not public enhancement. Before public enhancement, the repository needs a second wave to identify the best supported existing route, confirm content maturity, authority, boundary, evidence, source-rights, fair-housing, responsive, route, Search, and map posture, and define a no-new-route public-copy scope.

### New Public Neighborhood Routes

Finding: remain premature.

New public routes still require separate public-route authorization, registry eligibility, sitemap and canonical governance, Search support, map/boundary posture, public content standards, source-rights readiness, evidence completeness, fair-housing review, deterministic checks, local browser review, and production certification.

### Niwot Governance Reconciliation

Finding: viable future governance-only work, not the next primary implementation.

Niwot is better framed by the certified architecture, but remains unresolved for public treatment. It should not be activated as a city guide, Local Decision Intelligence candidate, public neighborhood/submarket route, registry entry, Search target, or map object without a separate governance reconciliation that determines whether the correct posture is unincorporated community, CDP/community, market-area context, neighborhood/submarket object, or unresolved.

### Gunbarrel Governance Reconciliation

Finding: remain blocked.

No reviewed repository evidence resolves Gunbarrel's object-type ambiguity, city/neighborhood/community/market naming overlap, route and registry ambiguity, Search ambiguity, map boundary posture, or fair-housing risk. Gunbarrel should remain protected, non-public, route-ineligible, registry-ineligible, Search-ambiguous, map-blocked, and unauthorized for Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4.

### Local Decision Intelligence Wave 4

Finding: remain paused.

Wave 4 should not proceed until object-type certainty, route readiness, Search support, evidence readiness, source rights, customer demand, certification cost, and duplication with Neighborhood / Submarket intelligence are resolved. Potential candidates such as Niwot, Brighton, Firestone, Frederick, Thornton, and other governed communities are not cleared by this review.

### Second Property / Seller Evidence Phase

Finding: defer.

Another bounded internal phase could eventually deepen advisor-use readiness or link standards to Seller Readiness, Home Worth, or Property Intelligence, but not before there is a concrete authorized operating surface. Property / Seller Evidence should remain internal and should not activate lookup, providers, uploads, customer-specific packets, valuation, condition conclusions, or public evidence.

### Embedded Domain Evidence Integration

Finding: embed evidence inside the selected geographic domain.

Evidence Depth and Property / Seller Evidence should not become a generic standalone program. Evidence integration should accompany the second Neighborhood / Submarket wave by strengthening candidate evidence, source-rights, freshness, conflict, limitation, and public-use posture for geographic readiness.

### Product Experience Targeted Follow-Up

Finding: not justified as the next primary initiative.

No reviewed production evidence identifies a specific neighborhood discoverability, city-to-neighborhood continuity, property-to-neighborhood continuity, advisory handoff, CTA, mobile, duplicate-path, cognitive-load, or conversion-continuity defect that outranks the next internal geographic readiness step.

### Other Repository-Supported Priority

No other repository-supported initiative materially outranks a second governed Neighborhood / Submarket wave.
